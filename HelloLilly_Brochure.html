Opening

Executive Summary

The reason for this tool is simple. Actually succeed at helping each jobseeker with what they really need, on their own terms. One needs help with execution. One needs help with confidence. Others need motivation, energy, connections, research, self-awareness, AI know-how, social-media skills, or a systematic approach. The right help depends on the person, not on which coach they happened to meet, and not on whether the person happens to match the advice they were given.

Much of what fills a jobseeker's day is formalia. Building a CV, drafting a cover letter, searching adverts, researching companies, writing outreach, managing social media, sounding confident in an interview. These are execution and sales skills, not the actual qualities or expertise the person brings to a job. Where the formalia is necessary, AI and tools should carry it efficiently, so the jobseeker's energy goes to showing who they really are. And the coaches are freed for what only a human can do: motivate, advise, uplift, and help the person work through whatever is genuinely in the way.

HelloLilly already has the human infrastructure that this work requires: coaches, participants across more than 60 municipalities, employer relationships, and the administrative system that holds it all together. What is missing is the layer above it. A connected, AI-supported system that meets each jobseeker on their own terms, helps them act between meetings, gives every coach access to the organisation's full knowledge, and turns every case into learning for the next person. This document describes that layer, what it is, why it works, and how it is built one phase at a time.

One has a flawless CV and freezes in every interview. One speaks four languages, just not the one the form is written in. One has applied to two hundred jobs and heard nothing back. One has not managed to open the laptop in a month. One is sixty and sure the phone will never ring. Every one of them is stuck, and every one is stuck for a different reason.

That is the gap. Not a missing feature, but a missing system. The model administers and advises, while the real work is left to the individual, to which coach they happened to meet, and to the gifts that one person happens to have. Nothing connects across people, and nothing accumulates over time.

Do not leave outcomes to chance and good fortune.

Optimise what can be optimised with technology.

Let each jobseeker be part of their own journey, in the way they need and want it.

Section 01

1. Background

HelloLilly is Sweden's largest provider of matching services, with offices in over 60 municipalities and a stated goal of matching 10,000 people to the right job or education by 2030. The funding is results-based, which means faster and better matches are not a nice-to-have. They are the business.

But delivering advice is no longer enough.

And yet much of the actual work, the doing between meetings and the reaching the right help at the right moment, still rests on the individual. The advice exists. Almost everything else is left to the person to carry.

A connected system is the missing piece.

A system like this turns HelloLilly from a service that tells people what to do into one that does it with them, learns what works, and gets better over time. From guidance into momentum.

Written from inside the program. This vision comes from sitting on the participant side while also seeing it through the eyes of someone who builds digital products for a living. Many of the tools described here I have already had to build for myself. The community synergies have been seen working in other groups, and the knowledge base growing larger than any single contributor. None of this is theoretical for me either. The encouraging part is that the gaps any model of this kind faces are now cheap to close.

Section 02

2. Everyone Is Stuck for a Different Reason

Help for people out of work has historically been built around a working theory of the typical jobseeker, someone with a certain shape of barrier who needs a certain shape of help. That theory holds for some people some of the time. But there is no typical jobseeker. Everyone who has not found work has not found it for their own reasons, and each one needs different help to unblock it. There are very nearly as many distinct situations as there are people in them.

That width is the real difficulty, and it is no one's fault. Coaches are individuals with specific gifts. One has spent years placing nurses and knows the field cold. Another understands what it is to job-hunt with autism. A third has a knack for restoring confidence in people who have lost it. By definition, no one person can be the right gift for every life that walks through the door, and not because anyone has done anything wrong, simply because that is the human limit. It is the same limit that means even trained psychologists find it hard to see the world from inside another person's life. We all read it from our own frame of reference.

A system can do something no individual mind ever could: hold the full width of human difference. The idea is a hive of knowledge that quietly absorbs what every coach knows and what every situation teaches, understands it, spreads it, and offers it back, so that any coach has the full library at their fingertips for the moments outside their personal expertise. That is not replacing the coach. It is freeing them to do what they are uniquely good at, and supplying them with what no individual could possibly hold alone.

There is a flip side worth naming. In a high-technology world, the technology itself is often the blocker. For many people the systems, forms, and tools are the barrier rather than the bridge. So part of the help is removing that friction entirely, and meeting people regardless of their comfort with technology, including those who fear it.

And the case is not only logical, it is evidenced. There is solid research showing that the right tools and structured assistance raise the likelihood of finding work, and separate research showing that job clubs, built on structure, peers, and accountability, do the same. The design here draws deliberately on both.

What makes the difference is the combination. A hive of knowledge that supports the coaches on one side, and a different set of tools, places, and community for the jobseekers on the other. Each is valuable alone. Together they reach the full width that no model has reached yet.

Section 03

3. The Problems

The Execution Problem

How it is: Coaching, by its nature, delivers advice and leaves the doing to the jobseeker. Advice, seminars, and tips all end the same way, with the person still having to execute, alone.

A theory worth sitting with. For many people in long-term coaching, the gap may not be advice at all, but execution. Sometimes that means the doing never quite happens. Other times the doing happens but in the wrong way: a flood of applications with little tailoring, quantity standing in for quality. They are two sides of the same coin. If that is true, then more advice is not the answer. Help to execute, and to execute on the right things, is.

Wrong skills, both sides. Getting a job and doing the job are different skills, and yet today the first decides access to the second. Sales skills and performing confidence should not be the deciding factor for a logistics manager or a truck driver to be hired for work they could clearly do. The same logic runs on the coach side. Admin, scheduling, and marketing copy are not what a coach is best at, or what their day should be made of. Both jobseekers and coaches are being asked to be good at the wrong things, and that is exactly the work the system should carry.

What is missing: Tools, support, community, easy access, and templates. For some people HelloLilly does not need to point the way. It needs to take their hand and lead.

The effect: Good advice never becomes action. Momentum dies between meetings, and 'it is up to you' becomes the exact point where progress stops.

Scenario: Your coach said to tailor your CV for each role. Sound advice. It is now Tuesday night, you have three roles open in browser tabs, and you have no idea how to start. So you do not.

The Consistency Problem

How it is: When help rests on individuals, what each person experiences inevitably varies, because every coach brings their own gifts and their own limits. It is a feature of the model, not anyone's fault.

What is missing: A consistent operational standard that does not depend on the gifts of the person each jobseeker happens to meet.

The effect: People with similar starting points may find themselves on very different paths. At scale, that variance becomes the largest hidden cost in any model built on individuals.

Scenario: You and a friend both join. You get a coach who is brilliant with CVs, your friend gets one whose gift sits elsewhere. A year later one of you has a job and neither of you knows it came down to who you happened to meet.

The Confidence Problem

This stands for inner barriers in general, not only confidence. And much of confidence is also quietly fixed by not leaving the person alone with the work. The CV shaped with help, the cover letter reviewed, the interview rehearsed, the post or message checked, each one stops the daily erosion and starts the rebuilding.

How it is: For many long-term jobseekers the real barrier is not knowledge, it is belief. Age, repeated rejection, ADHD, autism, and time out of work all erode confidence.

What is missing: A way to rebuild self-efficacy and to practise without being judged. The evidence is blunt here: structured job clubs, built on peers and accountability, place far more people than searching alone, and the system should emulate exactly that.

The effect: People who know exactly what to do still freeze. The barrier is internal, and more advice does not touch it.

Scenario: You have read every interview tip there is. You still feel sick the night before, you have nowhere safe to rehearse, and when the moment comes your mind goes blank.

The Relevance Problem

How it is: The help on offer can be genuinely good, and often is. But good is not the same as right for this particular person.

What is missing: A diagnosis of the real blocker. Most people do not actually know what is stopping them from finding a job.

The effect: Effort pours into the things that are already fine while the real obstacle goes untouched. Good help, aimed at the wrong target.

Scenario: Your CV is polished and your interviews feel okay, and still nothing lands. No one has worked out that the real problem is the roles you are applying for, not the way you are applying.

The Silo Problem

How it is: In any organisation built on individual expertise, knowledge naturally lives in the heads of the people who hold it. That is not a failing, it is simply how organisations work without a system that does otherwise. Each coach has a real gift, one knows headhunting cold, one is brilliant with nurses, one understands autism, but a gift in one head is not yet a gift the organisation can offer everyone.

What is missing: A way to make any coach's expertise available to every jobseeker who needs it, and a way for the organisation to keep that expertise even when a coach moves on.

The effect: A jobseeker may end up with a coach whose gift sits in another area, not because anyone made a mistake but because there is no system to detect the match. And when an experienced coach leaves, much of what they learned tends to move with them.

Scenario: You are a nurse, assigned to the coach who used to place senior executives. The coach who has placed forty nurses is two desks away, and you will never meet them.

The Barrier Problem

How it is: Different people are blocked by different things. Some struggle to write, held back by a second language, low confidence in Swedish, dyslexia, or simply hating to sell themselves. Others struggle to search at all, with no reliable internet or an attention span the job hunt quietly defeats. For some the technology itself is the wall, the systems and forms are the barrier rather than the bridge.

What is missing: Help adapted to the specific barrier, built on collected, quantifiable data rather than one coach's memory. With real data you can cross-link what works for whom, prove it, and improve the help step by step, instead of relying on coaches' knowledge alone.

The effect: Without collected data, the help on offer tends toward the average, and the people whose situations sit furthest from average find the least suitable fit. The system as a whole does not learn from those cases the way it could.

Scenario: Swedish is not your first language. The advice is good but it is all in fast Swedish, the forms assume fluency, and asking for things to be slowed down feels like admitting you do not belong.

Section 04

4. Why People Will Engage

This only works if people use it. Here is what brings each group in and keeps them coming back.

For jobseekers

  • You are not alone - see others in the same situation, and stop feeling like a failure
  • Always a clear next step - never the blank-page paralysis of not knowing what to do, the system always knows what the smallest useful action is
  • See people get out - watching others land jobs is proof there is a way through, and the motivation to keep going
  • A real community - open forums to ask, talk, and belong, not just a portal you log into
  • Help in one place - tools, tips, and templates, adapted to you
  • Share contacts and vouch - you know a carpenter, someone knows a photographer, someone knows a principal. Introductions, references, and honest opinions, the mechanic that works so well in startup incubators
  • Works for you whatever your tech comfort - two ways in: a light, spoken mode where you talk into a microphone and the assistant guides the next step, or full settings and writing for those who want control. It helps people who fear technology, not only those at ease with it
  • Coach-approved, in one place - knowledge, links, videos, discussions, and PDFs, with an assistant under every item so you can ask what a link or clip says without reading or watching all of it. The point is the approval: coaches vet and add it, jobseekers can suggest items too, but a coach signs off

For coaches

  • An internal system of their own - shared presentations, and each coach's CV, strengths, and background visible to every coach and to the assistant
  • Help analysing and answering - written help for hard questions, transcribed presentations, upvoted research, and summaries of discussions and coaching, all feeding a Sweden-specific knowledge base the assistant draws on
  • Meeting intelligence - with consent, live sessions and Teams or Zoom calls are transcribed and analysed, so help continues after the meeting and summaries are sharper
  • A living profile for every jobseeker - each person's history becomes a knowledge base the assistant uses to understand them better over time, and to suggest education, links, and contacts from the jobseeker pool
  • Coaching prompts that grow - as the knowledge deepens, the system flags when support, motivation, or another coach is needed
  • More time for the human work - the report writes itself and the tool carries the mechanical load, freeing even more time for the things only a coach can do: motivation, trust, and relationships

For HelloLilly

  • Faster, better matches - directly tied to funding and the 2030 goal
  • A higher quality ranking - a consistent standard across every office
  • Documented activity - generated automatically as a byproduct, not chased after the fact
  • Durable IP - coaching expertise captured as a company asset rather than lost when people leave
  • Easier to attract expertise - as the support around coaching grows, industry-specific knowledge becomes easier to recruit
  • A new kind of hire - with AI carrying the operational baseline, HelloLilly can bring in specialists rather than only generalist coaches: people who understand autism, ADHD, age, youth, newcomers, or disability, even if they are not traditional coaches
Section 05

5. The Solution

The skeleton is already there

The infrastructure for this work already exists. Providers in this sector, HelloLilly included, run on Workbuster Matcha (now part of Multisoft), and it does what it is built to do well: administration, storing the data, scheduling, the library of templates and links, and the documentation that has to happen anyway. That is the skeleton. It is real, it is paid for, and it should stay.

What infrastructure cannot do on its own is create outcomes. Stored data is not learning. A library is not advice. A scheduler is not a conversation. The value is added by what sits on top: an AI that works with the data, a community that connects the people, tools that help the jobseeker actually execute, and a learning layer that makes the whole thing get better over time. That is what this vision adds, not in place of the skeleton, but on top of it.

It also explains why the build is faster than it sounds: the foundation is already in place.

How the system answers each problem

Advice is everywhere and momentum is nowhere. Each of the six problems needs more than advice. It needs tools that do the work alongside the person. The map below shows the main tools for each problem from Section 3.

Execution

Consistency

Confidence

Relevance

Alongside each tool, a side panel surfaces what is relevant in the moment. Coach-approved templates, examples from similar roles, short videos on the question being answered, anonymised cases where consent was given, discussions where another jobseeker hit the same wall, the right coach for the situation, role and company research, or motivational notes. The mix depends on the problem and the task. What does not change is that the help appears next to the work, instead of being somewhere the person has to leave the task to find.

The crosslinking layer

The most important part of the system is not any single tool. It is the way every tool, person, resource, case, discussion, template, video, coach and piece of knowledge is crosslinked to whatever the jobseeker is doing at that moment. That is what turns a platform from a set of useful features into a living support system.

Most systems organise information by category. This one organises help by situation. The jobseeker should not need to know whether they need a template, a coach, a video, a discussion, a job search, a research note or an interview exercise. The system reads the context and connects the right support.

It is also where HelloLilly's long-term advantage becomes strongest. Individual AI tools are easy to copy. A crosslinked knowledge and support system, built from HelloLilly's own coaches, cases, resources, discussions, outcomes and labour market knowledge, is much harder to copy. The tools are the visible layer. The crosslinking is the system.

Example: CV Builder. The system recognises that the person is describing previous responsibilities. It can suggest better ways to describe measurable results, examples from similar roles, a short guide on writing achievements, a coach-approved template, a video on how recruiters read CVs, common mistakes in that role, relevant job titles to search for, missing keywords, a coach with experience in that field, and, where people have given explicit consent, anonymised examples from similar cases. The help appears because it is relevant to the task, not because the person had to search for it.

Example: Interview Trainer. The system uses the job description, CV, company research and previous practice answers to generate a realistic interview. At the same time it crosslinks to common questions for the role, company-specific research, advice from coaches, examples of strong answers, confidence-building exercises, follow-up questions based on weak answers, contacts connected to the company, and coaches who know the industry. The person is not just practising. They are practising in a connected environment that understands the role, the company, themselves and the support around them.

Example: Job Radar. When a signal points to a company expanding in the person's target region, the system does not only show the company as interesting. It crosslinks the signal to the person's CV, suitable roles, possible blind application angles, relevant contacts, outreach suggestions, coaches with knowledge of the company, recent news, possible interview preparation, and the person's Company List. A market signal becomes an action path.

The best of what already works

Each part of this is borrowed from something proven, then connected and built for HelloLilly.

  • From a great coach: judgment, encouragement, and the right next step. Solves the Execution Problem.
  • From the best CV tools: clean structure and awareness of how applications are screened. Solves the Execution Problem.
  • From an interview coach: rehearsal and honest, kind feedback. Solves the Confidence Problem.
  • From a job club: structure, accountability, and peers, the things the evidence says actually move outcomes. Solves the Confidence and Progress Problems.
  • From a community: shared templates, tips, and help that anyone can contribute. Solves the Relevance and Silo Problems.
  • Built for HelloLilly: tied to the coaches, the employer network, and the matching mission. Solves all of them, because it is not generic.

Why the small things matter

Often it is not the big steps that block people, but the small ones, the tasks that take far too long and still come out worse than they should. Simple tools do not find anyone a job on their own. What they do is even the road, clearing the friction so a person's energy goes to what actually matters. Finishing a CV, getting a cover letter done, finding a usable photo, testing a CV, sending outreach, working out what to write on LinkedIn. None of it is the goal, all of it gets in the way, and which tasks are hardest is different for every person.

Section 06

6. The Unique Value

The tools are easy to copy. The expertise is not.

Any single tool here is a weekend's work over a language model. That is not where the value is. The value is the engine underneath: a knowledge base built from your own coaches' real work, weighted by what actually got people hired.

  • Expertise that compounds - every coach interaction is captured and makes the system more capable over time
  • Outcome-weighted - it learns not what coaches say, but what works, because it can see which advice preceded a real placement
  • A flywheel competitors cannot catch - more coaches means a richer base, more clients means more outcome data, more outcome data means sharper weighting, and a rival starting from zero has none of your cases

Human relationships still beat AI

As AI floods the job-search market with generic tools, the human coach becomes more valuable, not less. The system augments the coach, it does not replace them. The humans do the part only humans can: motivation, trust, and accountability.

Why HelloLilly can own this

Generic AI tools are available to everyone. What makes this defensible is everything HelloLilly already has that a generic startup does not.

  • Many jobseekers, many coaches, many municipalities, many different barriers and life situations
  • Real employer relationships and a results-based business model that rewards good matching
  • Existing administrative infrastructure to build on, not replace
  • Activity and outcome data accumulating already
  • Recurring patterns across cases that no individual AI vendor can see
  • A stated 2030 goal that gives the strategic horizon

Everyone has access to AI. HelloLilly's advantage is not AI alone. It is AI connected to HelloLilly's coaches, cases, network, resources, Swedish labour-market knowledge and outcomes.

One value layer, built for the mission

Above the existing infrastructure, the value layer lives in one place, built around HelloLilly's actual job, which is matching people to the right work. The skeleton and the layer are not in competition. One holds the data and the workflow. The other turns them into outcomes.

Section 07

7. How We Build It Responsibly

  • Augment, never replace. The coach stays the anchor of the relationship.
  • Human in the loop. People review what matters before it reaches a jobseeker.
  • Compliant by design. GDPR and the EU AI Act, with data handled inside the Arbetsformedlingen framework.
  • Continuously learning, the right way. Start with an off-the-shelf model and make it better over time by feeding it your own Swedish data through a living, cross-linked knowledge base. That keeps it current the moment content is added, lets it cite its sources, connects the local world of names, companies, cities, and rules, and means it can forget a person on request. Selective fine-tuning comes later, only where it clearly earns its place, such as tone or a language nuance.
  • Consent first. Any recording or transcription of sessions and calls happens only with clear, informed consent, and a person can decline without losing support.
  • Honest scope. Job search aggregates public sources only, and the application fit check is a keyword and format check, not access to a real applicant tracking system.
  • Transparent. A jobseeker can see what the system knows about them and ask why a suggestion was made.
  • Suggestions, not decisions. The system surfaces options and reasons. People decide.
  • Support, not surveillance. Activity logging exists to help the person remember, restart, and recover. It is not a monitoring tool.

On the community side specifically, not everything is shared with everyone. The system has clear boundaries between public templates and guides, coach-approved resources, anonymised examples shared with explicit consent at the point of capture, private case data visible only to the person and their coach, and coach-only discussions. The community is real. The privacy is also real.

Section 08

8. The Full Vision

This is the complete suite, organised by what each tool helps with. Each one is useful on its own and ships independently. The categories make the shape of the system visible; the connected layer described earlier is what makes them work together.

A. Execution tools

Help the jobseeker get the actual doing done.

CV Builder (CV-byggare)

Why: Get past the blank page.

  • Works step by step. Does not need to produce the perfect CV immediately. The point is a usable first version that can be sent, improved and adapted over time
  • Starts with experience, education, strengths, achievements and goals, then gradually improves wording, structure, keywords, role fit and different versions for different types of jobs
  • Removes the barrier that stops many people before the application process even begins

Cover Letter Builder (Personligt brev)

Why: Write relevant cover letters based on the job advert, the CV and the person's situation.

  • More than generic letters. Helps handle common concerns or weaknesses such as gaps in the CV, age, career change, limited Swedish, disability, low confidence or fear of saying the wrong thing
  • Helps the person express themselves more clearly, and also dare to send the application

Application Check (Ansökningskoll)

Why: Compare the application with the job advert and show what matches, what is missing and what can be improved.

  • Honest about what it is: a practical fit check, not a promise about how a real employer or applicant tracking system will judge the application
  • Concrete output: missing keywords, weakly answered requirements, strengths to lift, parts of the CV or letter to adjust, what should be improved before sending
  • Helps the jobseeker learn from every application instead of sending many weak ones without understanding why they did not work

Progress Support (Framstegsstöd)

Why: A support tool for the jobseeker, with a part called My Activity (Min aktivitet) inside the system.

  • Reminds, suggests, motivates and breaks larger tasks into smaller steps. For people with low energy, ADHD, autism, anxiety, language barriers or long-term unemployment, the next small step can be the difference between progress and paralysis
  • Activity is logged to help the person, create overview and simplify reporting. The tone is supportive throughout

Interview Trainer (Intervjuträning)

Why: A realistic interview simulation, not just a list of common questions.

  • The jobseeker adds the job description, and the system creates a likely interview based on the role, company, industry, CV and normal interview questioning
  • Style is adjustable: friendly, formal, detailed, case-based, competency-based, short and direct, or more reflective
  • Asks follow-up questions depending on the answer, because real interviews are not static questionnaires
  • With AI-generated voices, the experience resembles modern language-learning tools, where the person practises speaking in a realistic but safe environment before the real meeting
  • The aim is not only better answers. The aim is safety, confidence, familiarity and reduced fear

Image Studio (Bildstöd)

Why: Help people create or choose better profile images and visual material for CVs, LinkedIn and professional contexts.

  • Simple, safe and template-driven, especially for people who find it hard to present themselves visually
  • Not about creating a false version of the person. About helping them appear clear, professional and confident

B. Opportunity tools

Find work, both visible and hidden.

Job Search (Jobbsök)

Why: Gather jobs from multiple public sources in one place.

  • The jobseeker can choose which sites to search, add new sources, change the search and receive help improving it
  • Not passive. The system learns from what the person clicks, saves, rejects and searches for, and suggests better terms, roles, industries and locations
  • Shows the latest incoming jobs, sorted by relevance, match, location, industry or desired role

Match Analysis (Matchanalys)

Why: Each job carries a match percentage, but the explanation is more important than the percentage.

  • Why a job seems suitable, why it may not be, which requirements are missing, which parts of the CV to highlight
  • Whether the role is worth applying for now, or whether another role, title or industry may be a better target
  • Turns job search into a learning process: the jobseeker begins to understand why some jobs are better targets than others
  • Helps the system optimise the search itself, with better keywords, alternative titles, related industries or a more useful geographic area

Job Radar (Jobbradar)

Why: Find opportunities before they appear as ordinary job adverts.

  • Monitors public signals: news, company pages, press releases, hiring signals, expansions, tenders, investments, leadership changes, new offices
  • Creates weekly lists such as companies growing in a specific region, companies likely to hire in a specific field, companies that recently won new contracts, companies that received investment, and companies suitable for blind applications
  • When a company is identified, shows whether anyone in the jobseeker's network or any coach within HelloLilly has a connection, experience or previous cases linked to it
  • Turns a market signal into an action path

Company List (Företagslista)

Why: The jobseeker's own wish list of interesting employers.

  • Built manually or suggested by the system based on CV, experience, geography, interests, industry, match data and Job Radar signals
  • For each company: why it may be relevant, which roles may fit, what recent signals exist, which contacts may be useful, whether HelloLilly has relevant coach knowledge, whether a blind application may be suitable

Blind Applications (Spontanansökningar)

Why: Contact companies even when there is no published role.

  • Fewer but better attempts: more relevant, more personal and better timed, not generic mass outreach
  • Company research, finding a suitable contact, writing the first message, preparing the CV angle, planning follow-up, logging what has happened
  • Makes proactive job seeking possible even for people who would normally be too uncertain to try

C. Preparation tools

Help the jobseeker show up ready.

Research Helper (Researchstöd)

Why: Do research correctly, relevantly and in the right amount.

  • Templates for different situations: before applying, before an interview, before a blind application, before a career change, before contacting someone professionally, before choosing between different jobs or industries
  • Summarises companies, industries, role requirements, competitors, news, culture, products, possible interview questions and useful contact angles
  • Helps the person tell what is relevant from what is just noise

Interview Prep (Intervjuförberedelse)

Why: Connects research, job advert, CV and Interview Trainer into structured preparation for a real interview.

  • What the company does, what the role requires, which questions may come, which examples from the CV to use
  • Risks or weaknesses to prepare for, questions the jobseeker can ask, which answers need more practice
  • Makes interview training specific rather than generic. The person practises the right things for the right meeting

D. Network tools

Activate people, contacts and community.

LinkedIn Helper (LinkedIn-stöd)

Why: A safety and confidence tool for people who find social visibility, outreach and LinkedIn communication difficult.

  • For many jobseekers, the problem is not lack of willingness. It is fear: of writing the wrong thing, seeming strange, being too pushy, saying something stupid, being judged
  • Helps the person check, improve and understand the communication before anything is sent or published
  • Creates the feeling: 'This is not wrong. This is reasonable. This is professional. This has been checked'
  • Helps with profile, connection requests, first messages, follow-ups, comments, post ideas, tone, clarity, industry fit and recipient fit
  • Not automation. Help to communicate with more safety, clarity and relevance

Outreach Plan (Kontaktplan)

Why: Plan professional contact with employers, recruiters, former colleagues, industry people and possible introducers.

  • Who to contact, why, in what order, with what message, when to follow up, what to avoid, how to keep the communication natural
  • Especially important for people who struggle with unwritten social rules
  • Connects to Company List, Job Radar, Network Match and LinkedIn Helper

Network Match (Nätverksmatch)

Why: Show whether there is any connection between the jobseeker, coaches, HelloLilly's network and a specific company or industry.

  • Whether someone in the person's own network is connected to the company
  • Whether a coach knows the company or has helped others into similar roles
  • Whether another jobseeker has previous experience there, where that person has given explicit consent for their case to be shared
  • Whether there are relevant templates, examples or tips
  • All handled with privacy, consent and clear boundaries. A person who feels alone suddenly has access to a wider support network

E. Coach tools

Support the coaches with everything outside their own personal expertise.

Case Record (Ärendevy)

Why: The shared picture of the jobseeker's journey, not only an internal CRM.

  • Intake answers, goals, barriers, CV versions, applications, interview practice, saved jobs, company list, research, coach notes where appropriate, agreed next steps, documents, summaries, activity history
  • Both coach and jobseeker can see and understand the current situation, goals, barriers, activities and next steps
  • One living shared picture, instead of a journey scattered across meetings, memories, documents, emails and different systems

Live Support (Mötesstöd)

Why: Help the coach during and after meetings.

  • With clear consent, sessions can be recorded, transcribed and summarised
  • The assistant supports the coach with follow-up questions, relevant tips, similar previous cases, links, tools and suggested next steps
  • After the meeting, the system can document what was agreed, send relevant resources and update the Case Record
  • Similar live documentation and assistant patterns are already used in highly sensitive environments such as healthcare. Consent, access control, data security, human oversight and clear boundaries are designed in from the start
  • Never replaces the coach's judgement. Helps the coach listen, remember, document and connect the person to the right support

Coach Network (Coachnätverk)

Why: Make HelloLilly's internal competence searchable.

  • No coach can be an expert in everything. One is strong in healthcare, another in youth, another in autism or ADHD, another in academic roles, another in sales, logistics, industry or management
  • If a jobseeker is a biologist and the coach has limited experience in science, the system can suggest another coach with relevant background for a second opinion
  • Solves a practical problem and a strategic one. The total competence of the organisation becomes available to every coach and every jobseeker

Coach Review (Coachgranskning)

Why: Ask several coaches for feedback on a CV, cover letter, LinkedIn profile, image, application or strategy.

  • Supportive and constructive, not judgmental
  • More perspectives without forcing the jobseeker to start over with a new contact
  • Especially useful when a case requires specialist knowledge or when the person is stuck even though a lot has already been tried

Knowledge Hub (Kunskapshubb)

Why: Templates, links, presentations, guides, videos, documents, FAQs, coach experience and user tips, all in one place.

  • Quality assurance: jobseekers can suggest material, coaches approve and organise it
  • Under each resource, an assistant that can summarise, explain and help the person use the material without having to read or watch everything
  • Turns knowledge into something active and usable, not just stored

Feedback Loop (Återkoppling)

Why: Help HelloLilly learn from users and coaches.

  • Internal polls, questions, suggestions, comments, upvoted resources, improvement ideas, feedback after interviews, seminars or applications
  • Lets the system develop from real needs, not assumptions
  • When many people get stuck at the same point, HelloLilly can see it and improve the support

F. Learning layer

Turns experience into intelligence over time.

Knowledge Hive (Kunskapssystem)

Why: The long-term core of the system, where HelloLilly's experience accumulates.

  • Gathers knowledge from coaches, resources, meetings, presentations, research, discussions, cases, applications and outcomes
  • Over time, sees which advice, tools, contact paths and strategies more often lead to real progress
  • What competitors will find hardest to copy. Individual AI tools are easy to create. A knowledge system built on HelloLilly's own coaches, target groups, cases, data and outcomes is a strategic asset

Outcome Engine (Resultatmotor)

Why: Connects actions to results.

  • Which support helps which people. Which activities lead to interviews. Which coach interventions create momentum. Which tools are used
  • Which paths lead to jobs or education. Which jobseeker groups need different support. Which advice should be repeated and which should be improved
  • Not reducing people to statistics. Letting the organisation finally learn systematically from the work already being done every day

G. Delivery surfaces

Where the system lives.

The hub

Why: Everything above needs a calm home, not a dense dashboard.

  • One clear next action at a time
  • Visible, motivating progress
  • Quiet entry points to every tool

An app on the phone

Why: A surprising amount of the value of any tool like this is that it sits in the pocket and quietly nudges the person when they need it.

  • Downloadable on iOS and Android, the whole suite in one place
  • Reminders for new matching jobs, applications that need finishing, scheduled coach calls, seminars, and guidance sessions
  • Works for people who live more on a phone than at a desk, which is most people
Section 09

9. Build Order

Nothing here needs to be built all at once. Each tool is useful on its own. The phases below sequence the suite so that early value is real, and the connected system grows around it.

Phase one: Foundation (Grundsystem)

Tools that create immediate value and are relatively easy to build.

  • CV Builder (CV-byggare)
  • Cover Letter Builder (Personligt brev)
  • Application Check (Ansökningskoll)
  • Progress Support (Framstegsstöd)
  • Case Record (Ärendevy)
  • Knowledge Hub (Kunskapshubb)

Direct value to jobseekers and coaches without requiring the full vision to be built first.

Phase two: Matching (Matchning)

Strengthens job search, matching and preparation.

  • Job Search (Jobbsök)
  • Match Analysis (Matchanalys)
  • Interview Trainer (Intervjuträning)
  • Research Helper (Researchstöd)
  • Interview Prep (Intervjuförberedelse)

The system moves from helping with material to helping with direction, selection and practice.

Phase three: Network (Nätverk)

Activates networks and relationships.

  • LinkedIn Helper (LinkedIn-stöd)
  • Outreach Plan (Kontaktplan)
  • Company List (Företagslista)
  • Blind Applications (Spontanansökningar)
  • Network Match (Nätverksmatch)
  • Coach Network (Coachnätverk)
  • Coach Review (Coachgranskning)

Many opportunities are not reached through ordinary applications alone. They come through contact, timing, trust and the right introduction.

Phase four: Market and learning (Marknad)

Makes the system more proactive and self-improving.

  • Job Radar (Jobbradar)
  • Live Support (Mötesstöd)
  • Feedback Loop (Återkoppling)
  • Knowledge Hive (Kunskapssystem)
  • Outcome Engine (Resultatmotor)

HelloLilly is no longer only helping people respond to job adverts. The system also reads the labour market, identifies hidden opportunities and improves itself over time.

What not to build first

Showing judgement about what to skip on day one matters as much as choosing what to do. Several things in the Full Vision are powerful but only earn their place once the foundation exists. These are intentionally left for later phases, not the first wave.

  • The full Outcome Engine
  • Advanced Live Support, beyond a simple session summary
  • Full network mapping
  • A polished native mobile app, a responsive web app is enough to start
  • Any fine-tuned models
  • Heavy data integrations beyond public sources
  • Automated outreach to employers

Start with the tools that create immediate value: CV Builder, Cover Letter Builder, Application Check, Progress Support, the Case Record and the Knowledge Hub, plus simple crosslinking. Everything else grows from there.

Why this is realistic

The first versions are now fast and inexpensive to prototype. With today's AI models, templates, data connections, transcription, search, voice tools and document generation, many modules can be built quickly in a first version. The strategic value, though, lives less in the first tool and more in the connected system, the accumulated knowledge, and the outcome-weighted learning over time.

Several tools can also start as simple prototypes. CV Builder, Cover Letter Builder, Research Helper, Application Check, LinkedIn Helper and Interview Trainer can begin as narrow tools with clear templates and improve as real users begin to use them.

The point is not to build everything at once. The point is to start with a structure where every module can grow into the same connected system.

The tools help the individual. The connected system, built on HelloLilly's own coaches, cases, and outcomes, helps the organisation get better every day. That is the difference, and that is what is missing.
Appendix

Appendix A: Why This Works

The design is not a guess. It is built on what the evidence says actually helps people get back to work. These findings come from structured programs and human services, so they are the case for the human-plus-tool model, not for software alone.

  • The right tools raise the odds. Across many studies, structured job-search interventions and assistance raise the odds of obtaining employment roughly 2.67 times (Liu, Huang and Wang, 2014).
  • So do job clubs. Separately, classic job-club trials, built on structure, daily activity, peers, and accountability, placed far more people than unstructured search. Two different routes, both evidenced, and this design draws on both.
  • Confidence is causal, not soft. The JOBS II program improved both re-employment and mental health by building a sense of mastery and inoculating people against setbacks, with the largest gains for those most at risk.
  • For barrier groups, place then train. Supported employment, which places people quickly and supports them afterwards, roughly doubles competitive employment for people with significant barriers.
  • Speed matters. The longer someone is unemployed, the harder re-entry becomes, so anything that restores momentum early works against that scarring.
  • Different situations, different paths. Each finding above applies to different people in different ways. Some are helped most by tools and structure, some by peers and accountability, some by place-then-train, some by confidence and resilience. There is no single funnel that fits everyone, and the evidence itself says so.

The design follows from that. Instead of analysing a jobseeker and pushing them into a predetermined path that may or may not address the real problem, the system lets the person's situation find the right help. The help comes to the person, not the person to a funnel.

Appendix B: A Week in the Life

Sara is 52 and has been out of work for fourteen months. Her confidence is low and she has started to avoid the search altogether.

Monday. She opens the hub. It does not show her a wall of tasks, just a warm greeting and one next step: finish the CV she started. The assistant asks her about her last role in plain questions, one at a time, and the CV fills in as she talks. She did not have to face a blank page.

Wednesday. A role appears in her aggregated search. The assistant drafts a cover letter and, because Sara worried about her age, quietly frames her experience as the strength it is. The application fit check flags two missing keywords and she adds them.

Thursday. She is nervous about an interview. She practises out loud with the trainer, three times, alone in her kitchen, and the feedback is kind and specific. By the third try she does not freeze.

Friday. Her coach opens Sara's file and the activity is already logged, so the report is done. Instead of admin, the meeting is about how Sara is feeling and who the coach knows at the company. The tool did the mechanical work. The human did the human work.

Three weeks later Sara has an offer. The system did not replace her coach. It made sure that everything between the meetings actually happened.
