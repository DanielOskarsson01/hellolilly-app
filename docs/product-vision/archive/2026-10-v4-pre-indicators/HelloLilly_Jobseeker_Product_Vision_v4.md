## Opening

> Get the job because you are good at it.
> Not because you are a designer, a copywriter, a search ninja, a marketing whiz, a social media manager or a recruitment expert.
> And not because you are overly positive, endlessly energetic, naturally self-confident or an extremely motivated workaholic with thousands of connections.
>
> **Let HelloLilly's digital tools be all of that, so you can concentrate on what you are good at.**

People should get work because of their skills, talent and expertise in their own field. Today, too often, they get it because they happen to be good at the job hunt. And the people who come to HelloLilly through Rusta och matcha are rarely the overly positive, endlessly networked self-promoters the job hunt rewards. That is not a flaw to fix in them. It is a gap that a connected set of digital tools can close, and those tools are relatively easy to build. Several of them already run.

The reason for this system is simple. Actually succeed at helping each jobseeker with what they really need, on their own terms. One needs to understand their situation and their options. One needs a placement to get a foot in the door. One needs an education, and does not know it can be free. One needs someone to vouch for them. One needs help with execution, one with confidence, one with a plan that holds together. The right help depends on the person, not on which coach they happened to meet, and not on how comfortable they are with technology.

Most people who are stuck are not stuck on one thing. They are stuck on the space between things: between the advice and the doing, between the job advert and the course that would qualify them, between the coach who knows the field and the jobseeker who needs that coach, between the contact who could open a door and the person who does not know the contact exists. Every one of those gaps is carried today by the individual, and the people least able to carry them are the ones furthest from work.

**HelloLilly does not need another job-search tool. It needs the infrastructure that connects the help that already exists.** Information, job search, work placements, education and its funding, LinkedIn and personal networks, recommendations, coaches, the other jobseekers and what they know, a career plan that is analysed rather than filed away. One system that holds them together around one person, so that a jobseeker without digital confidence can spend their energy on the things that matter, and not on finding their way through forms, portals and tabs.

**The promise is simple: a baseline in a week.** Within the first week, every jobseeker should have what today takes months to assemble, or never happens at all: a clean CV and a findable profile, an application engine that finds, sorts and prepares jobs every morning, a plan for education where that is the honest route, a short list of the right people to reach, and a place among others in the same situation. None of it depends on the person being a marketer, a designer, a search expert, a social media manager or an extrovert, and none of it depends on which coach they happened to meet. That baseline is not the goal. It is the floor from which the real work starts: the personal approach, the better application, the follow-up, the idea nobody else had, the door a coach opens. The tools make up for the skills a person only needs to get a job, so they can show the skills they bring to the job itself.

The work is divided three ways. **The system carries the load. The person makes the choices. The coach does the human work.** Agents search, research, draft, check, remind and connect. The person decides, approves every word and sends. And the coaches, freed from admin and from being the only route to everything, become what they actually are: HelloLilly's largest and most under-used asset.

HelloLilly already has the human infrastructure this requires: coaches with real expertise and real networks, participants across more than 60 municipalities, employer relationships, and the administrative system that holds it all together. What is missing is the layer above it.

One has a flawless CV and freezes in every interview. One speaks four languages, just not the one the form is written in. One has applied to two hundred jobs and heard nothing back. One has not managed to open the laptop in a month. One is sixty and sure the phone will never ring. One spent twenty years in a trade that is thinning out. One would be hired tomorrow if anyone had seen them work for a week. Every one of them is stuck, and every one is stuck for a different reason.

> Do not leave outcomes to chance and good fortune.
> Optimise what can be optimised with technology.
> Let each jobseeker be part of their own journey, in the way they need and want it.

## 1. Background

HelloLilly is Sweden's largest provider of matching services, with offices in over 60 municipalities and a stated goal of matching 10,000 people to the right job or education by 2030. The funding is results-based, which means faster and better matches are not a nice-to-have. They are the business.

**But delivering advice is no longer enough.** The help a jobseeker needs is spread across many places: adverts on many sites, rules and rights across several authorities, courses in a national catalogue few people know exists, funding with application windows that open and close, contacts in other people's address books, and expertise in the heads of coaches in other offices. Advice tells a person these things exist. Almost everything else, finding them, understanding them, combining them and acting on them, is left to the person to carry.

**What changed is what AI agents can now do.** A year ago, an AI could help write a paragraph. Today, a set of agents can search for jobs every morning, read every advert, research the employer, check which courses close a gap and whether they are free, prepare a funding application before the window opens, explain an official letter in plain language, find the right person to contact at a company, and prepare an interview, all before the person has had breakfast. The hard part is no longer producing the material. It is making sure every word is true, that the person stays in charge, and that the help reaches the people who need it most rather than the ones already confident with technology.

**The technology itself is often the wall.** Every new portal, form and app raises the floor a jobseeker has to stand on. For the people furthest from work, the digital load is not a side issue. It is frequently the barrier that hides all the others. A connected system can lower that floor instead of raising it: one place, one next step, spoken if needed, with a coach one tap away.

**A connected system is the missing piece.** A system like this turns HelloLilly from a service that tells people what to do into one that prepares the work with them, connects them to every route that exists, learns what works, and gets better over time. From guidance into momentum.

**Written from inside the programme.** This vision comes from sitting on the participant side while also seeing it through the eyes of someone who builds digital products for a living.

**Several of these ideas are not drawings. They already run, in the author's own tooling.** A daily pipeline finds jobs, scores them for fit, tailors a CV into the right version, drafts the cover letter and runs an automated consistency check before anything is sent. An interview preparation tool researches the company, analyses the job and the recruiter or hiring manager, lists the gaps to fill, the experience to highlight and the questions to ask, anticipates the likely questions with strong answers, and presents it all as a dashboard that can be used live during an online interview, or as preparation and a printout for one in person. And a LinkedIn people search, post search and contact vault, built and running for another platform the author operates, already does what the outreach, LinkedIn and coach-network parts of this vision need. Appendix C describes what runs. The tools are therefore not a hope. The work is to turn a process that needs a technical person at a terminal into a form that anyone can use, which is exactly what HelloLilly is for.

## 2. Everyone Is Stuck for a Different Reason

Help for people out of work has historically been built around a working theory of the typical jobseeker, someone with a certain shape of barrier who needs a certain shape of help. That theory holds for some people some of the time. But there is no typical jobseeker. There are very nearly as many distinct situations as there are people in them.

That width is the real difficulty, and it is no one's fault. Coaches are individuals with specific gifts. One has spent years placing nurses and knows the field cold. Another understands what it is to job-hunt with autism. A third has a knack for restoring confidence. A fourth knows half the warehouse managers in the region. No one person can be the right gift for every life that walks through the door. It is the same limit that means even trained psychologists find it hard to see the world from inside another person's life.

A system can do something no individual mind ever could: hold the full width of human difference, and connect each person to the help that fits them. A hive of knowledge that absorbs what every coach knows and what every case teaches, and offers it back, so that any coach has the full library and the full network of the organisation at their fingertips.

### Who comes through the door

The research names the groups that are hit hardest, and the coaches meet all of them:

| Who | What stands in the way |
|---|---|
| **Has given up** | Applies only to keep the benefit. Years of rejection have become learned helplessness, and more advice does not reach it. |
| **Autistic or introverted** | Good at the work, but the job hunt rewards selling, small talk and cold outreach, the parts with least to do with the job. |
| **Older** | Callbacks fall from the early forties onwards, more steeply for women. Another course is not the answer to bias; positioning and relationships are. |
| **New to Sweden** | Language, qualifications that are not recognised here, and discrimination. |
| **Forced to change** | A trade that closed or is thinning out. The wellbeing cost can last for years, even after a new job. |
| **Low digital confidence** | No computer at home, and every portal assumes one. Digital-only help widens the gap. |
| **Young, outside work and school** | Overlapping risks, and services that never join up. |
| **Back from long sick leave** | Unsure how much they can manage, and unsure what support exists for finding out. |
| **Freezes in interviews** | A flawless CV and a blank mind when it matters. |
| **Applies everywhere** | Two hundred applications and nothing back. Volume brings rejection; targeting works. |

One finding matters more than any other here: structured help works best for the people who start with the least motivation and the lowest belief in themselves. The person who has given up is not a lost cause. They are exactly who the system is for.

The person's challenge is rarely a single one. It moves across fronts, and the fronts are connected:

| The front | What the person is facing |
|---|---|
| **Understanding** | What are my rights, my options, my support? What does this letter mean? Who do I even ask? |
| **Direction** | What should I aim for? Is my plan realistic? Is my old trade still there? |
| **Finding work** | Where are the jobs, including the ones never advertised? Which are worth my energy? |
| **Getting a foot in** | Can I get a placement, a work trial, a chance to show what I can do? |
| **Learning** | Which education closes my gap? Is it free? Can I get paid while I study? Can what I already know be recognised? |
| **Presenting myself** | CV, letter, LinkedIn, photo, interview: all the selling that has nothing to do with doing the job. |
| **Connecting** | Who do I know, who do they know, and who would recommend me? |
| **Each other** | Who else is in my situation, and who has the skill I need: a photographer, a designer, someone who knows AI, a lawyer, an entrepreneur? |
| **Keeping going** | Energy, confidence, structure, and not being alone with it. |

Today each front has its own place, its own login and its own logic, and the person is the only thing connecting them. The system's job is to be that connection instead.

And the case is evidenced. Structured job-search assistance raises the odds of finding work; job clubs built on structure, peers and accountability do the same; the human counsellor is the most reliably effective ingredient; and digital-only help widens the gap for people with low digital confidence. The design draws deliberately on all four.

## 3. The Problems

### The Execution Problem

- **How it is:** Coaching, by its nature, delivers advice and leaves the doing to the jobseeker. Advice, seminars and tips all end the same way, with the person still having to execute, alone. Getting a job and doing the job are different skills, and yet the first decides access to the second. Sales skills should not decide whether a logistics manager or a truck driver is hired for work they could clearly do. The same logic runs on the coach side: admin, reporting and scheduling are not what a coach is best at, or what their day should be made of.
- **What is missing:** Something that does the preparation work alongside the person. For some people HelloLilly does not need to point the way. It needs to take their hand and lead.
- **The effect:** Good advice never becomes action. Momentum dies between meetings, and "it is up to you" becomes the exact point where progress stops.
- **Scenario:** Your coach said to tailor your CV for each role. Sound advice. It is now Tuesday night, you have three roles open in browser tabs, and you have no idea how to start. So you do not.

### The Navigation Problem

- **How it is:** The support a jobseeker is entitled to is spread across authorities, municipalities, schools, unions and websites, each with its own rules, language and timing. Free education, study funding, work trials, wage support for employers, study and careers guidance: much of it exists, and much of it goes unused because nobody connected it to the person at the moment it mattered.
- **What is missing:** One place that explains the person's own situation in plain language, knows which routes exist for someone like them, and turns that into a next step.
- **The effect:** People miss what they are entitled to. The support that exists reaches the people best at finding it, not the people who need it most.
- **Scenario:** A letter arrives full of terms you do not understand. Someone mentions a course "that might be free", and someone else a grant "with a window in the spring". You search, open six tabs, give up, and decide it is probably not for people like you.

### The Digital Problem

- **How it is:** Every tool meant to help assumes a certain comfort with screens, forms, logins, uploads and written language. For many people the technology itself is the wall: a second language, dyslexia, low digital confidence, no computer at home, or simply fear of doing something wrong.
- **What is missing:** Help that works whatever the person's comfort with technology: spoken, one question at a time, on the phone, in plain language, and with a coach able to do it with them.
- **The effect:** The most digital help reaches the most digital people. The ones furthest from work fall further behind, and the system quietly widens the gap it exists to close.
- **Scenario:** Your coach sends a link to a great tool. It asks you to create an account, upload a document and choose from forty options. You close it, and next week you say you did not have time.

### The Confidence Problem

This stands for inner barriers in general. Much of confidence is quietly fixed by not leaving the person alone with the work: the CV shaped with help, the letter checked, the interview rehearsed, the message reviewed.

- **How it is:** For many long-term jobseekers the real barrier is not knowledge, it is belief. Age, repeated rejection, ADHD, autism and time out of work all erode confidence.
- **What is missing:** A way to rebuild self-efficacy and practise without being judged, with peers and structure around it, the way job clubs do.
- **The effect:** People who know exactly what to do still freeze. More advice does not touch it.
- **Scenario:** You have read every interview tip there is. You still feel sick the night before, you have nowhere safe to rehearse, and when the moment comes your mind goes blank.

### The Relevance Problem

- **How it is:** The help on offer can be good, but good is not the same as right for this person. Most people do not know what is actually stopping them, and the plan written at the start is rarely tested against what the market says.
- **What is missing:** A diagnosis of the real blocker, and a career plan that is analysed against real demand, real requirements and the person's real evidence, then revised as things are learned.
- **The effect:** Effort pours into things that are already fine while the real obstacle goes untouched.
- **Scenario:** Your CV is polished and your interviews feel okay, and still nothing lands. No one has worked out that the problem is the roles you are aiming for, not the way you apply. Your plan says "warehouse or retail" and has said so for nine months.

### The Direction Problem

- **How it is:** For some people the barrier is not how they present who they are. The work they know is thinning out, or the role they want needs a skill they do not yet have. Today the honest answer, a new direction or new skills, is exactly where the help usually ends.
- **What is missing:** An honest path: a realistic target built on what the person already knows, recognition of what they can already do, the education that closes the gap (often free), the funding that exists for exactly this, and applying directly kept as a real choice beside studying.
- **The effect:** People keep applying into a shrinking corner of the market, or leap into long study on hope rather than evidence. Those most exposed to change are the least likely to find the support and the money on their own.
- **Scenario:** You have driven a truck for twenty years and the adverts now ask for certificates you do not have. Someone says "maybe retrain?" and hands you a course catalogue. Nobody tells you which direction is realistic, that the course is free, that you may be paid while studying, or whether applying anyway is the smarter move.

### The Network Problem

- **How it is:** Many jobs are filled through a recommendation, an introduction or a placement that turned into a job, not through an advert. Long-term jobseekers often have the thinnest networks, the least practice in using them, and the greatest fear of asking. For introverts, and for anyone who finds social contact hard, reaching out to a stranger is not a skill gap. It is a wall. And a LinkedIn profile that is empty or out of date quietly closes the door before anyone knocks.
- **What is missing:** A way to see who the person already knows, find the right people at target companies and in target roles, approach them in small, safe steps, build a LinkedIn presence that works for them, get vouched for by people who have seen their work, and borrow the reach of HelloLilly's own coaches. For the hesitant, the whole thing has to feel like a comforting copy-and-paste exercise, not a performance.
- **The effect:** The person competes only in the most crowded channel, the open advert, where a gap in the CV counts most and who they are counts least.
- **Scenario:** Your former colleague's sister is the hiring manager at the company you most want to work for. You will never find out, because nobody ever asked who you know, and you would not have known how to ask her anyway.

### The Isolation Problem

- **How it is:** Hundreds of jobseekers in the same programme face the same walls, write the same kinds of letters, look for the same kinds of contacts and ask the same questions, each one alone. Among them are photographers, designers, people who know AI, former lawyers and entrepreneurs, whose skills another jobseeker needs this week.
- **What is missing:** A place where jobseekers find each other: to ask and answer, to share templates, outreach ideas and tutorials, to ask for a connection, and to offer and find expertise.
- **The effect:** Every person reinvents what the person next to them already solved, and the skills sitting idle in the programme are never used. The loneliness of the search compounds the confidence problem.
- **Scenario:** You need a decent profile photo and help with a portfolio. Three people in your programme are out-of-work photographers, and one is a designer. None of you will ever know about the others.

### The Dormant Potential Problem

- **How it is:** HelloLilly's coaches hold years of expertise, specialist knowledge and professional networks. Today almost all of it is reachable only by the jobseekers who happen to be assigned to them, and much of their time goes to admin and reporting. When a coach leaves, what they knew leaves with them.
- **What is missing:** A way to make every coach's expertise and every coach's reach available to every jobseeker who needs it, without exposing anyone's private contacts, and to give coaches back the time for the human work only they can do.
- **The effect:** The organisation's largest asset runs at a fraction of its capacity. People with similar starting points end up on very different paths, depending on who they met.
- **Scenario:** You are a nurse, assigned to the coach who used to place senior executives. The coach who has placed forty nurses, and who knows the head of the ward you want, is two offices away, and you will never meet them.

## 4. Why People Will Engage

This only works if people use it. Here is what brings each group in and keeps them coming back.

### For jobseekers

- **One place for everything** - jobs, placements, courses, funding, contacts, your plan and your coach, instead of a dozen sites and logins.
- **You understand your own situation** - your rights, options and support explained in plain language, and any official letter explained on request.
- **Always one clear next step** - never the blank-page paralysis of not knowing what to do.
- **Your morning starts with the work already done** - new jobs, placements and courses found overnight, each with the reason it was suggested. You decide; the system prepares.
- **Nothing is ever said in your name that you did not approve** - you press send yourself, every time.
- **Free education made visible** - the courses that close your gap, which of them cost nothing, and whether you can be paid while studying.
- **A way in through people** - who you already know, who knows the company, and coaches who can open a door for you.
- **Every answer is kept** - what you tell the system once is reused everywhere, so you never explain the same thing twice.
- **Works whatever your tech comfort** - talk instead of type, one question at a time, on your phone, or sit down and do it together with your coach.
- **A LinkedIn that works for you** - a profile built from your CV, a simple posting plan, and contacts made one small, safe step at a time, with every message ready to copy and paste.
- **You are not alone** - a community of people in the same situation: questions answered, templates and tutorials shared, connections requested, skills swapped, and stories of people who got out.

### For coaches

- **Time back for the human work** - the report writes itself, meetings are summarised with consent, and the mechanical load is carried by the system.
- **Your experience goes where it helps most** - not only to the people the draw and your office happened to bring you.
- **The whole organisation behind you** - every colleague's expertise searchable, a second opinion one request away.
- **Borrow from every colleague** - templates, outreach ideas and approaches that worked, shared and credited to the coach who made them.
- **Your network becomes a superpower, and stays yours** - your contacts stay on your own computer; the system can tell when one of them could open a door for a jobseeker, and you decide whether to make the introduction.
- **A living profile of every jobseeker** - goals, plan, activity, applications, courses and contacts in one shared view, without chasing.
- **Prompts that grow** - the system flags when support, motivation, a specialist or another coach is needed, and when a plan has stopped moving.
- **Your expertise counts** - what you know is captured, credited and reused, instead of disappearing when you change office.

### For HelloLilly

- **Faster, better matches into work or education** - directly tied to funding and the 2030 goal.
- **A consistent standard** across every office, independent of who a person happened to meet.
- **The full value of every coach**, not only the share their own caseload can reach.
- **Documented activity** generated as a byproduct, not chased after the fact.
- **Durable IP** - coaching expertise and outcome knowledge captured as a company asset.
- **A new kind of hire** - with the system carrying the operational baseline, HelloLilly can bring in specialists who understand autism, ADHD, age, youth, newcomers or disability, even if they are not traditional coaches.

## 5. The Solution: One System, Many Routes

### The skeleton is already there

Providers in this sector, HelloLilly included, run on Workbuster Matcha (now part of Multisoft). It does what it is built to do well: administration, storing the data, scheduling, the library of templates and links, and the documentation that has to happen anyway. That is the skeleton. It is real, it is paid for, and it should stay.

What infrastructure cannot do on its own is create outcomes. Stored data is not learning. A library is not advice. A scheduler is not a conversation. The value is added by what sits on top: agents that do the preparation work, routes that connect the person to every kind of help, a human layer that puts the right coach and the right contact in reach, and a learning layer that makes the whole thing better over time.

### The mechanism: the division of labour

Everything in this system rests on one division of labour, applied the same way in every route:

| | Who | Does what |
|---|---|---|
| **The system carries the load** | AI agents and the connected platform | Search, research, explain, draft, check, remind, assemble applications and funding packs, find the right course, contact or coach. Often overnight. |
| **The person makes the choices** | The jobseeker | Keep or cut, accept or change, apply or study, send or wait. Nothing is sent, posted or submitted in their name. Every decision is kept, so the next round starts from more than the last. |
| **The coach does the human work** | The coaches, all of them | Motivate, judge, encourage, open doors, sign off the big decisions, and help with whatever is genuinely in the way. |

The first line removes the formalia. The second keeps the person in charge of their own life and their own words. The third is where outcomes are actually made, and it is the part a system can multiply but never replace.

### One foundation, shared by every route

Every route draws on the same small set of shared things, so that nothing the person does is ever done twice:

- **My profile and fact bank.** Everything the person has told the system or confirmed: experience, education, skills, achievements, languages, preferences and wording. Built once through a guided career intake, extended by every answer, reused by every tool.
- **My plan.** The person's own career plan: goal, direction, routes being tried and next steps. Not a document filed at the start, but a living plan the system analyses against the labour market and the person's evidence, and the coach reviews.
- **The case record.** The shared picture of the journey, which the person and their coach both see.
- **Lilly and Help right now.** The assistant the person says hello to, and a column of widgets beside every page that shows the jobs, courses, people, discussions, templates and coaches related to exactly what is on the page. Only real items; nothing can be paid for.
- **Progress and activity.** One next step at a time, and a record of what has happened, kept to help the person remember and restart, never to monitor them.

### The routes

On top of the foundation sit the routes, one for each front a person may be stuck on. A person can use one, several or all of them, in any order, and every route feeds the others.

| Route | The person's question | What the system carries | Where the coach comes in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Understand my situation** | What are my rights, options and support? | Plain-language explanations, letters explained, the routes that exist for someone like me | Clarifies, reassures, and takes over where judgement is needed |
| **Plan my direction** | What should I aim for, and does my plan hold? | Plan analysis against real demand and evidence, realistic adjacent directions | Reviews and signs off the plan |
| **Find work** | Where are the jobs, seen and unseen? | Daily search, scoring, employer research, tailored applications, checks | Second opinion on targets and material |
| **Get a foot in** | Can I show what I can do? | Placements, work trials, staffing agencies, employers who take people on with support | Opens doors with employers HelloLilly knows |
| **Learn and re-skill** | Which education, is it free, and can I afford it? | Recognition of prior learning, matching courses, free routes, funding packs | Signs off any long education |
| **Present myself** | How do I come across? | CV, letter, a LinkedIn profile built from the CV, a LinkedIn posting plan, photo, all checked | Reviews in their own words |
| **Connect and get recommended** | Who can open a door or vouch for me? | My network map, target lists by company, industry and role, a step-by-step outreach plan with ready-to-send messages, matches to coaches' networks | Makes the introduction personally |
| **Help each other** | Who else is in my situation, and who has the skill I need? | Questions and answers, shared templates, outreach ideas and tutorials, connection requests, an expertise exchange | Approves shared material, moderates, joins in |
| **Prepare and practise** | How do I walk in ready? | Interview research, preparation pack, spoken rehearsal | Rehearses the human part |
| **Keep going** | How do I not stop? | One next step, reminders, community, visible progress | Motivation and accountability |

### No digital gate

The system is built for the person who fears technology, not only for the person at ease with it. That is a design rule, not a feature:

- **Talk instead of type.** Every route can run as a spoken conversation, one question at a time, each with a short explanation and an example.
- **Plain Swedish.** Every jobseeker needs, and has, a working level of Swedish. What they do not need is the dense language of authorities and official help sites. The system explains everything in plain, simple Swedish, which is easier and more helpful than what the person meets elsewhere.
- **Do it together.** Any route can be run side by side with a coach, in the meeting, on the coach's screen, with the person deciding. Some people will never open the app alone, and that is fine.
- **Mobile first.** Every tool is designed for the mobile first, installable as an app with reminders, for people who live more on their mobile than at a desk.
- **Digital skills as a route, not a requirement.** Learning the basics, from email to video calls to using AI, is offered as help in its own right, never assumed.

### The crosslinking layer

The most important part of the system is not any single tool. It is the way every tool, person, resource, case, coach, course, contact and piece of knowledge is crosslinked to whatever the jobseeker is doing at that moment. Most systems organise information by category. This one organises help by situation. The person should not need to know whether they need a template, a coach, a course, a contact, a funding rule or a video. The system reads the situation and connects the right help. **The tools are the visible layer. The crosslinking is the system.**

**Example: one answer, used everywhere.** An advert asks for experience training new staff. The person's profile does not mention it, so the system asks one question: "Have you ever trained someone new at work?" The person answers out loud: two summers onboarding seasonal workers. They accept the wording. That fact now appears in the next tailored CV, the cover letter can draw on it, interview preparation turns it into a story to rehearse, the plan analysis counts it toward a team-lead direction, and the match for every similar job improves. One answer, given once.

**Example: from a gap to a free course to a placement.** Match Analysis shows that most care-assistant adverts in the person's area ask for a certificate they do not hold. The system first checks whether what they already know can be formally recognised, then shows the two tuition-free courses that close the rest, which one runs near them, whether study funding applies, and what it means in months and money. Beside it, always, the option to apply now anyway. The person picks a short course. Progress Support keeps one light job action a week in the plan while they study, and when the course includes a placement, the system crosslinks it to the employers on the person's Company List and to the coach who knows those employers.

**Example: from a company to a recommendation.** The person adds a company they would love to work for. The system shows who in their own network works there or nearby, people at the company by role, a sequence for reaching them naturally, and, quietly, whether any HelloLilly coach's network reaches it. One coach's does. That coach makes the introduction. The person's message, drafted with help and sent by them, now arrives with a recommendation behind it.

**Example: one job page, everything around it.** On a job the person likes, Help right now fills in around the advert. The employer research is ready. Maria, a shift manager there, is in the person's own contacts, and one tap asks quietly whether a coach's network reaches the company too. The site manager and two team leaders appear from their public profiles, with the site manager's post last week about a new shift system as a natural first comment. The system has picked Karin as the coach who knows logistics and this employer best, and the person can ask her directly. And the forklift course that closes the gap starts in November. Nothing had to be searched for.

**Example: from a question to a skill swap.** A jobseeker asks in the community how to write to a hiring manager they have never met. Two people answer with the openers that worked for them, which are saved as templates and appear in the outreach tool for everyone. In the same thread someone mentions they need a profile photo; a member who was a photographer before offers a session, and in return gets help with their own CV from someone who used to work in HR. Nobody had to know who to ask.

**Example: from a CV to a LinkedIn presence.** Once the CV is built, the LinkedIn Profile tool turns the same confirmed facts into a headline, an About section and experience entries written the way LinkedIn is read. The LinkedIn Plan then proposes one simple post every week or two, built on something only the person knows, and the outreach tool suggests five relevant people a day to connect with. When the person publishes a post about what they learned on a course, the system suggests tagging the teacher and the classmate whose question started it. Their networks see it.

**Example: Interview Training.** The system uses the advert, the CV, the employer research and earlier practice answers to run a realistic spoken interview, while crosslinking to common questions for the role, coach advice, follow-up questions on weak answers, and coaches who know the industry. The person is practising in an environment that understands the role, the company and themselves.

### Why the small things matter

None of it is the goal, all of it gets in the way, and which tasks are hardest is different for every person.

## 8. How We Build It Responsibly

Three principles carry the system, and each is enforced in how it is built, not only promised:

- **The person decides what is true.** No line reaches a CV, letter or application unless it comes from something the person has confirmed or written. An application may present real experience at its strongest. It may never invent.
- **The writer never checks its own work.** The agent that drafts is never the agent that checks.
- **Argue for the person, never judge them.** When something falls short, the fault lies in the document, the gap or the market, never in the person.

Around them: **the system prepares, people decide**, and nothing is sent in anyone's name. **Consent first** for any recording or transcription. **Retrieval, not training**: the system draws on a living knowledge base that cites its sources and can forget a person on request, and personal data is never used to train a model. **A fork, not a funnel**: where re-training is raised, applying directly is always shown beside it. **Dated facts**: rules, amounts and windows are shown with their date and source, and never quoted from memory. **No digital gate**: every route works spoken and together with a coach. **Reach the underserved on purpose.** **Transparent**: every person can see what the system knows about them and delete it. **Support, not surveillance.**

Networks stay with their owners. A coach's contacts stay on the coach's own computer; a jobseeker's network map is built from their own exports and belongs to them. When the system finds people or companies, it uses public information only.

**Before real people use it**, the system passes a review of GDPR, the EU AI Act and the Arbetsförmedlingen framework, signed off by a qualified professional. Until then no claim of compliance is made.

The full set of principles, and the list of what the system will never do, are in **Addendum A: Principles and Boundaries**.

## 9. The Full Vision

This is the complete suite, organised by route. Each tool is useful on its own and ships independently; the shared foundation and the crosslinking are what make them one system. Every tool follows the same division of labour: the system carries, the person chooses, the coach does the human work.

### A. The foundation

**Career Intake (Karriärprofil).** The start of everything. The person loads what they have: old CVs, letters, certificates, links, or nothing at all. Then the system asks one question at a time, spoken or written, each with a short explanation and examples. Where the answer is already in the loaded material, it is suggested for the person to confirm. The result is the person's profile and fact bank, which every other tool draws on.

**CV Builder (CV-byggaren).** Builds the master CV and versions for the kinds of work the person is aiming for, from confirmed facts only, output as PDF or Word. A nurse's versions differ from a manager's; some people need sections others do not.

**My Plan and Plan Analysis (Min plan, Plananalys).** The person's own career plan, kept alive. The system analyses it against what the labour market says and what the person's evidence supports: is the target realistic here, what do adverts for it actually ask for, what is missing, which routes are open (apply, placement, course, network), and what the next three steps are. It flags when a plan has not moved for weeks, or when the market and the plan have drifted apart. The coach reviews it with the person; the person owns it.

**Case Record (Ärendevy).** The shared picture of the journey: goals, plan, CV versions, applications, placements, courses and funding, contacts, agreed next steps and activity, in one page the person can read too.

**Lilly and Help right now (Hjälp just nu).** Lilly is the assistant the person says hello to. She explains any tool, finds the way, answers questions about the person's own situation by pointing to what is actually in it, and hands over to a coach when judgement is needed. Beside every page, Help right now shows a column of widgets that follow what is on the page: on a job, the employer research, who has contacts there (the person's own, and quietly the coaches'), the people at the company and what they have posted lately, the coach the system judges to know this job or employer best, and the course that closes the gap; on a CV step, the template, the clip and the right coach. Items are matched on meaning against both the page and the person's situation, closed jobs and passed deadlines never appear, discussions are shown as the people in them, and a widget with nothing real is not shown. Nothing in the widgets can be paid for. Lilly suggests; she never acts on her own and never reads the person's emotions.

**Progress Support (Framstegsstöd).** Reminds, suggests and breaks large tasks into small ones, one next step at a time. Activity is logged to help the person remember and restart, and every item can be hidden. No streaks, no nagging. During any period of study it keeps one light job action a week in the plan.

**The hub and the phone.** A calm home, not a dashboard: one clear next action, visible progress, and quiet entries grouped by route. An installable phone app with reminders for new jobs, placements, course and funding deadlines, and coach calls.

### B. Understand my situation

**Situation Guide (Min situation).** The rules, rights, support and actors around the person, explained in plain language for their own circumstances: what they are entitled to, who does what, which routes exist for someone like them, and what the next step is. Every fact is dated and sourced; nothing is quoted from memory.

**Letter Helper (Brevhjälpen).** The person photographs or forwards a letter or message from an authority, an employer or a school. The system explains what it says, what it asks and by when, adds any deadline to the plan, and points to the coach where a judgement is needed. It explains; it never replies on the person's behalf.

**Tutorial Library (Filmbanken).** HelloLilly's internal bank of tutorials and short video clips on everything around the search: how to job-seek, how to plan, how to handle age-related challenges, how to use LinkedIn, how to prepare for an interview, how to use the digital basics. Made by coaches, specialists and jobseekers who have been through it, approved by a coach before it is shared, and short enough to watch on a phone. Lilly can summarise any clip, and the Help right now panel shows the right one next to the task.

**Knowledge Hub (Kunskapshubb).** Templates, guides, documents and coach experience in one place, alongside the Tutorial Library. Jobseekers can suggest items, which stay marked as suggested until a coach approves them. Lilly can summarise or explain any item, so nobody has to read or watch everything.

**Research Helper (Researchstöd).** Research a company, an industry, an occupation or a course, with a short summary of what matters for the purpose and a "dig deeper" on any point. Templates for the moments that need it: before applying, before an interview, before a career change, before contacting someone, before choosing between two options.

**Digital Skills (Digital vardag).** Short, practical, spoken-friendly help with the digital basics the job search assumes: email, uploading a document, video calls, using a phone for applications, using AI safely. Offered as help in its own right, linked to the free courses and library support that already exist.

### C. Find work

The full application route is described step by step in Appendix C. In short:

**Job Applications (Jobbansökan).** A morning start page with jobs found overnight, sorted by how likely the person is to like each one and how well they fit, each with the reason. A filter that learns from the person's own reasons. An employer brief for each chosen job. A tailored CV and cover letter from confirmed facts only. A gap interview that asks one question when an advert wants something the bank does not hold. Three reviewers who read the application as a screening system, a recruiter and an HR consultant would. A final truth check. The person sends, and the application appears in **Ansökningskoll**, where every sent application shows its status.

**Application Questions (Ansökningsfrågor).** Many application forms ask questions of their own: why this role, a time you solved a problem, when you can start, what salary you expect. On the job's own page, where the tailored CV and cover letter are made, a chat window sits beside them. The person pastes the employer's question, or asks one of their own. The tool reads the person's confirmed material (fact bank, CV, earlier answers and the advert) and suggests an answer in the person's own voice, at the length the form allows. When the material holds no answer, it says so and asks. The person writes or speaks a rough answer, and the tool turns it into a clear one without adding anything the person did not say. The person copies the answer into the form; nothing is sent for them. Every answer they keep goes into their own answer bank, so the next form that asks the same thing takes seconds, and the same answers feed Interview Preparation, where the questions often come back out loud. This already runs in the author's own tooling; the spoken answer is new.

**Job Radar (Jobbradar).** Opportunities before they become adverts: companies hiring more in the person's region and field, relevant news, and further signals over time, each with one suggested action.

**Company List (Företagslista).** The person's own wish list of employers, each card showing why it is relevant, open roles, signals, contacts and status, and the launch point for research, outreach and blind applications.

**Companies by Area (Företag i området).** Every employer within a region or commuting distance, so the person sees the whole map around them rather than finding companies one advert at a time.

**Blind Applications (Spontanansökningar).** Contact companies with no published role, in five steps: research, angle, recipient, message, follow-up. If the person feels stuck, it first asks how much they can manage today, and "not today" is a valid answer.

### D. Get a foot in

**Placement Finder (Praktik och prova på).** Work placements, work trials, internships and supported first jobs, matched to the person's direction. It shows where employers can receive public support for taking someone on, so the person can raise it in the conversation, and it crosslinks every option to the employers HelloLilly already works with and the coach who knows them. A placement is treated as a first-class route into work, not a consolation prize.

**Staffing Agencies (Bemanningsföretag).** A curated guide to staffing agencies, often the fastest route into work, with direct links and a CV prepared for each. The person sends.

**Test Before You Invest (Prova först).** A short plan to try a direction for real before committing to it: an informational conversation, a day of shadowing, a taster course, a work trial. Each step says what is being tested and what would change the person's mind.

**Employer Bridge (Arbetsgivarbryggan).** The coach-side link to HelloLilly's employer relationships. When a person is ready for a placement or a first job in a field, the coach sees which employers the organisation works with there, and makes the approach personally.

### E. Learn and re-skill

Together they form one guided flow, the **Omställning Guide (Omställningsguiden)**, from "why are you here?" to a plan, built around the course-fit engine behind the Education Matcher. The tools are reached from Match Analysis or Plan Analysis, when a requirement cannot honestly be met yet, or directly, by a person who arrives without a target. They open by asking why the person is changing, because the right help differs sharply between a chosen move, a redundancy and a health reason. And whatever the distance, one choice never leaves the screen:

| Apply now anyway | Re-train |
|---|---|
| Faster, a lower fit. Often the honest better choice: rapid placement with support beats studying first for many people. Always shown, never buried. | Slower, a higher fit. Worth it when the skill distance is large, and only with the timeline named: returns arrive over years, not months. |

**Transition Compass (Omställningskompassen).** A short, spoken-capable conversation that records why the person is changing, their financial runway, their comfort with technology and their confidence. For someone on sick leave, it surfaces the formal routes back to work as a matter of course.

**Direction Explorer (Riktningsutforskaren).** Maps the person's real experience onto the labour market's own map of occupations and skills, and offers a few realistic adjacent directions, each with what transfers, what is missing, how far it is, demand, live adverts and salary reality. Ends in something to test, not a verdict.

**Prior Learning Check (Validering).** Before recommending any course, checks whether what the person can already do could be formally recognised, so nobody studies for a year to prove what they already know.

**Education Matcher (Utbildningsmatchningen).** Ranks the few real courses that close the gap, from the national catalogue of publicly funded education: vocational college, adult education, folk high school, university and labour-market training. It shows plainly which are tuition-free, which are short, and which include a work placement, with an honest outcome note, the true cost and timeline, and applying directly beside it. Shorter, demand-aligned training is offered first where it fits.

**Free Learning (Fri kunskap).** Free ways to learn that do not require enrolling anywhere: open online courses, language learning, digital and AI basics, and short certificates employers recognise, matched to the person's gap and direction, so a missing skill can start closing this week.

**Funding Navigator (Finansieringsguiden).** Checks eligibility for study funding against the real conditions, shows amounts and dates as dated facts, and assembles an application-ready pack in advance, with a motivation the coach reviews, so the person is not beaten by an incomplete form or a slow start. Those who will not qualify are routed to the alternatives that fit them, and to the free, independent study and careers guidance every municipality already offers.

**Transition Economics (Omställningskalkylen).** Applying now, a short course and a full re-training side by side, in months and money, under the real support caps. Payback is named honestly, in ranges, never as a promise.

### F. Present myself

The LinkedIn tools are separate and offered only where they fit the person's field, region and wishes. A plumber in a small town may need none of them; most people need only a findable profile; some want more. "Not for me" is a valid answer.

**LinkedIn Profile (LinkedIn-profil).** Builds the person's LinkedIn profile from the CV, which is why the CV Builder comes first. The same confirmed facts are rewritten the way LinkedIn is read: a headline that says what the person does and wants, an About section in the first person, experience entries with results, the right skills, and a photo from Image Studio. Nothing appears that is not in the person's fact bank. The person copies each part into LinkedIn, field by field, guided step by step.

**LinkedIn Plan (LinkedIn-plan), optional.** A simple media plan for the person's own LinkedIn, built on what works on the platform: original posts from the person rather than shared links, at most one post a day and usually one every week or two, each containing something only the person knows (a lesson from a course, an observation from twenty years in a trade, a question they are genuinely curious about) and ending with a real question rather than an engagement bait line. Posts that name and tag a real person travel further, so the plan suggests whom to mention: the teacher who explained something well, the person who asked the good question, the author of a post worth building on ("as Anna put so well..."). It also schedules short, thoughtful comments on other people's posts, which cost nothing in reach and are where most new contacts start. The person writes in their own words; the system helps shape, shorten and check, and never posts.

**LinkedIn Helper (LinkedIn-stöd).** A safety and confidence tool for anything the person writes on LinkedIn: profile text, connection requests, messages, comments and posts. It shows a suggestion beside what the person wrote, with a plain explanation of why it reads better, and ends in one verdict: *send*, *send with one change*, or *hold*. After *send* it offers nothing more, because permission to stop is the point. It never posts.

**Image Studio (Bildstöd).** Help the person look clear and professional in a CV or profile photo, under one rule: *same face, better photograph*. The person approves the before and after.

**Coach Review (Coachgranskning).** Ask one or several coaches for feedback on a CV, letter, profile, plan or application, answered in the coach's own words. No AI ever writes in a coach's voice.

### G. Connect and get recommended

Many jobs are filled through a recommendation, an introduction or a placement, not an advert. This route builds that path on purpose, for the confident and, above all, for the hesitant.

**Network Map (Nätverkskarta).** Who the person already knows and where those people work, to find warm ways into target employers. It covers more than LinkedIn, because many people's real networks live elsewhere, and it is built from the person's own exports, owned by them.

**Contact Planner (Kontaktplaneraren).** A personal CRM built for people who find reaching out hard. It works in four steps:

- **Choose the targets.** Companies the person wants to work at, industries they want to work in, or roles they want to have. Each target can be added at any time, and contacts can be linked to a job after the fact.
- **Find the people.** For each target, the people who matter, grouped by role: senior leaders, hiring managers, HR, recruiters, future team members and respected people in the industry. Found from public profile and post information only, with what each person has posted recently, so there is always something real to respond to.
- **Choose the approach.** From a short menu of approaches, ordered from the gentlest: a thoughtful comment on something they posted; a simple connection request; a light first message ("I would love to work in your industry. Would you mind if I asked one simple question?"); a request for ten minutes of advice; and only later, once there is a relationship, a request for a recommendation or an introduction. The person picks the approach that feels possible today.
- **Follow the plan.** A small daily pace, for example five to ten people, with each next step prepared as a ready-to-send text the person reads, adjusts if they wish, copies and pastes. Replies are logged, follow-ups are reminded, and the person sees which approaches work for them.

The fear of social contact is real, and the tool does not ask anyone to perform. It turns the whole thing into a calm, guided copy-and-paste exercise, one small step at a time. The person always does the sending.

It is not limited to LinkedIn. For local trades the targets are local firms, staffing agencies and suppliers, reached by phone, email or a visit, with each step prepared as a short script or message.

**Network Growth (Nätverksbygge).** Alongside the targeted plan, a gentle daily habit of connecting with relevant people in the person's field, region and target companies, so the network grows steadily and every future search, post and recommendation reaches further.

**Ask for a Recommendation (Rekommendera).** People who have seen the person work, former colleagues, placement supervisors, coaches and course teachers, can be asked for a short reference or a LinkedIn recommendation through a simple, prepared request the person sends.

**Network Match (Nätverksmatch).** When a case needs a door opened at a company, the system checks quietly whether any coach's network reaches it. Each coach's contacts stay on the coach's own computer; only a yes or no ever reaches HelloLilly. On a yes, that coach decides whether to make the introduction personally. No names are shown, and nobody can search the network freely.

### H. Help each other

The other jobseekers are one of HelloLilly's largest unused resources. Many of them carry skills another jobseeker needs this week, and all of them have solved something someone else is stuck on.

**Community (Gemenskap).** The social heart of the system: a place to ask questions and get answers, to share what worked, to celebrate a placement or a job, and to keep each other going. Coaches take part, approve what is shared more widely, and moderate. Clear privacy boundaries between public, coach-approved, consented and private material.

**Shared Bank (Delningsbanken).** Templates, letters, outreach openers, LinkedIn posts that worked, interview answers and tutorials, shared by jobseekers and coaches, rated by the people who used them, and fed straight into the tools: an opener that worked appears as an option in the Contact Planner, a template in the CV Builder.

**Expertise Exchange (Kompetensbörsen).** Jobseekers ask for and offer expertise: a photographer, a designer, someone who knows AI or design tools, an entrepreneur who has started a company, a former lawyer, someone who has worked in HR. A person who needs a photo, a portfolio review or a second opinion on a business idea finds someone in the programme who can help, and using their own skill for someone else is good for the helper too. It is also a place to ask for connections: "does anyone know someone at this company?"

**Vouch for Each Other (Gå i god för).** The mechanic that works so well in startup incubators: members who have worked with each other in the exchange can recommend each other, share contacts and pass on leads, with consent at every step.

### I. Prepare and practise

**Interview Preparation (Intervjuförberedelse).** Already built and used for real interviews. For every interview or important meeting it delivers:

- **Research:** the company, its market and competitors, the real employer when a recruiter posted the advert, and recent news.
- **Analysis of the job:** what the role really needs, and an honest "can you do it, and do you want it?"
- **Analysis of the people:** the recruiter or hiring manager the person will meet, what they are likely to care about, and how to weight the conversation for each.
- **Gaps to fill and experience to highlight:** the honest gaps and how to bridge them, and the stories from the person's own experience that answer the role best.
- **Anticipated questions with strong answers:** the likely questions, simulated and answered from the person's own facts, ready to rehearse.
- **Questions to ask:** sharp, specific questions that show preparation.

Everything is presented as a dashboard, with the advert where clicking a phrase shows the person's angle and answer. In an online interview it sits open beside the call as live support. For an interview in person it is the preparation tool, and a printout to bring along. Afterwards, a short review offers anything new the person said as a fact to keep.

**Interview Training (Intervjuträning).** The tool plays the interviewer out loud and the person answers out loud, in a safe place, as many times as they like, built from the advert, the CV and the employer research. It asks follow-up questions, adjusts style, and gives feedback that quotes the person's own answer. The aim is not only better answers. It is safety, familiarity and less fear.

### J. Coach tools

The coach's own working day comes first. Before any network or sharing, every coach needs one place that keeps the caseload in order.

**Coach Workspace (Coachens arbetsyta).** The coach's daily home, built on the same case record the person sees:

- **Onboarding (Uppstart).** The first meeting run as a guided session. Coach and person start the Career Intake (Karriärprofil) together, spoken or typed, and begin the CV, so the week-one baseline begins in the first meeting rather than after it. Both are finished over the next few days, and the plan is agreed by day three, after which a few applications go out every day.
- **Plan follow-up (Planuppföljning).** The person's plan with its agreed steps, each marked done, not done or stuck, with who owns it: the person, the coach or the system.
- **Meetings and calendar (Möten och kalender).** Invitations to meetings in person, by video or by phone, reminders the person actually receives by text message or email, rebooking in one step, and the coach's calendar in one place.
- **Meeting log (Mötesanteckningar).** A light CRM for the coach: what was said last time, what was agreed, open follow-ups and how often the person has been met. Before each meeting it prepares a short brief: since last time, what is done, what is not, and what is new in the case. With consent, Live Support can fill it from the meeting itself.
- **Caseload overview (Översikt).** Every person on the caseload at a glance: what is done, what is not, what has stalled, what the system can take off the table now (a CV that can be built, applications the engine can prepare, a funding pack that can be assembled), and what needs the coach's own judgement. The coach sees where an hour of their time matters most.

The workspace records nothing for its own sake. Everything in it either moves the person's next step or saves the coach a task, and every note is part of the person's case record, which the person can read.

**Coach Profiles and Coach Network (Coachprofil, Coachnätverk).** Each coach's fields, special strengths, languages and background, searchable by every coach and by Lilly, so the coach who knows the field can give a second opinion or take a case.

**Coach Contacts (Coachkontakter).** Each coach loads their own professional contacts into a private, encrypted store on their own computer. HelloLilly never receives, stores or searches it. It powers Network Match while the contacts remain the coach's own. The vault tooling, including the download of a person's own LinkedIn connections and content, already runs on another platform the author operates.

**Live Support (Mötesstöd).** With clear consent, recorded before anything is processed, sessions and calls are transcribed and summarised, so agreed steps and resources reach the case record after the meeting.

**Coaching Prompts (Coachsignaler).** Flags when a plan has stopped moving, when activity drops, when a deadline is near, or when a specialist or another coach is needed. Prompts support the coach's judgement; they never feed decisions about benefits or eligibility.

**Transition View (Omställningsvyn).** Shows the person's direction, education, funding and placement plans to the coach, with prompts that follow the evidence: a tighter cadence where confidence is low, a wellbeing check where the change was forced, a positioning strategy where age or gender bias is in play. Any plan built on long education needs a coach's sign-off.

**Feedback Loop (Återkoppling).** Suggestions, upvotes, polls and feedback after interviews, placements, courses and applications, so HelloLilly sees where many people get stuck and improves the help there.

### K. Learning layer

**Knowledge Hive (Kunskapssystem).** The long-term core, where HelloLilly's experience accumulates: from coaches, resources, cases, placements, courses, applications and outcomes. What competitors will find hardest to copy.

**Outcome Engine (Resultatmotor).** Connects actions to results: which support helps which people, which routes lead to interviews, placements, education and jobs. Outcomes are recorded as dated facts, never as proof of cause. No figure ever describes fewer than five people, and nothing it learns ever feeds a decision about a person's benefits, sanctions or eligibility.

## 10. Build Order

Nothing here needs to be built all at once. Each tool is useful on its own. The phases sequence the suite so that early value is real across several routes, not only applications, and the connected system grows around it.

| Phase | Purpose | Tools |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Foundation** | One profile, one plan, one way in, for everyone | Career Intake, CV Builder, My Plan, Case Record, Coach Workspace (onboarding, plan follow-up, meetings and calendar, meeting log, caseload overview), Lilly and Help right now, Progress Support, Knowledge Hub and Tutorial Library, Community and Shared Bank, Situation Guide, Letter Helper, spoken mode and do-it-together mode |
| **2. Routes** | Work, placements and education side by side | Job Applications, Application Questions and Ansökningskoll, Plan Analysis, Placement Finder, Staffing Agencies, Transition Compass, Prior Learning Check, Education Matcher, Free Learning, Funding Navigator, Coach Review, Expertise Exchange |
| **3. People** | Opportunities that come through people, and the coaches unlocked | Coach Profiles and Coach Network, Coach Contacts and Network Match, Network Map, Contact Planner, Network Growth, Ask for a Recommendation, LinkedIn Profile, LinkedIn Plan, LinkedIn Helper, Company List, Companies by Area, Blind Applications, Interview Preparation and Training, Employer Bridge |
| **4. Market and learning** | A proactive, self-improving system | Job Radar, Direction Explorer and Transition Economics, Live Support, Coaching Prompts, Feedback Loop, Knowledge Hive, Outcome Engine |

The position behind this order: the foundation and the information route come first because they help every jobseeker, including the ones who will never apply for a job through a screen. Work, placement and education come next, together, because offering only one of them pushes people into the wrong route. The coach network comes in the third phase because it needs a pilot coach group and the legal review behind it, and it is the phase where the largest dormant value is released.

### The first version

The first version has two sides, because its first users are a pilot coach group. For the jobseeker it is the application baseline, where most of the work already runs or is built. For the coach it is the first slice of the Coach Workspace, which has to be built but reads case data the app already keeps.

| For | Step | Tool | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jobseeker | Build the CV | CV Builder | Reviewed; twelve agreed changes before building |
| Jobseeker | Find and match | Job Search, Match Analysis | Built in the HelloLilly app |
| Jobseeker | Apply | Tailored CV and Cover Letter, the before-you-send check, Application Questions | Built in the app; Application Questions already runs in the author's own tooling |
| Jobseeker | Track | Ansökningskoll | Next to design; the built screens already link to it |
| Jobseeker | Prepare | Interview Preparation | Runs today in the author's own tooling; moving it into the app is planned |
| Coach | Onboard and plan | Onboarding with the Karriärprofil, plan follow-up | To build; a demo screen exists |
| Coach | Meet and remember | Meetings and calendar, meeting log | To build |
| Coach | See the caseload | Caseload overview | To build, on the case data the app already keeps |

It delivers the first part of the week-one baseline, a clean CV and a running application engine, and it gives the pilot coaches a working day that keeps itself in order. It is not the whole foundation: My Plan, Lilly and the plain-Swedish guides follow next, so the system never becomes only a job-application tool. The coach network waits for the legal review.

### How it reaches real people

1. **A pilot coach group first**, working with the tools on sample cases, so professional users harden the system before the first vulnerable user arrives.
2. **Then a small group of consenting jobseekers**, using only the tools that are fully real, after the legal review is signed off.
3. **Then the wider rollout**, phase by phase.

Before the first real user: logins and roles, "see what it knows" and "delete me" all work.

### What we will measure

| Measure | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Placements into work or education, in HelloLilly's own verified terms | The 2030 goal |
| Time from first meeting to the week-one baseline (CV, profile, application engine, plan, first contacts) | The core promise, measured |
| Share of people who use more than one route (work, placement, education, network) | Whether the system connects, or is just a job-search tool |
| Time from a need being identified to a first action taken | The execution and navigation problems, in days |
| Entitled support actually taken up (study funding, free courses, placements) | Whether the navigation problem is being solved |
| Introductions made through coach and personal networks, and their outcomes | Whether the dormant potential is released |
| Questions answered, templates reused and skills exchanged between jobseekers | Whether the isolation problem is being solved |
| Share of coach time spent on human work rather than admin | The core promise to coaches |
| Uptake among the people furthest from the labour market, including low digital confidence | Whether the system reaches the underserved, not only the confident |
| Interviews per application sent | Whether tailoring and checking work |

A baseline for each is taken before the pilot starts.

### Why this is realistic

The first versions are now fast and inexpensive to build. With today's AI models and agents, transcription, voice, search and document generation, many tools can be built quickly. Several of the hardest pieces already run in the author's own tooling: the daily job pipeline from search to a checked, tailored application, answers to an application form's own questions, the interview preparation dashboard, and the LinkedIn people search, post search and contact vault running on another platform. The education and direction tools lean on open public data: the national course catalogue, the labour market's map of occupations and skills, and demand forecasts. The strategic value, though, lives less in any first tool and more in the connected system, the coaches it unlocks, and the outcome-weighted learning over time.

> The tools help the individual. The connected system, built on HelloLilly's own coaches, cases and outcomes, helps the organisation get better every day. That is the difference, and that is what is missing.

## Appendix A: Why This Works

The design is built on what the evidence says actually helps people get back to work. These findings come from structured programmes and human services, so they are the case for the human-plus-system model, not for software alone.

- **The right tools raise the odds.** Across many studies, structured job-search interventions and assistance raise the odds of obtaining employment roughly 2.67 times (Liu, Huang and Wang, 2014).
- **So do job clubs.** Classic job-club trials, built on structure, daily activity, peers and accountability, placed far more people than unstructured search.
- **The human counsellor is the reliably effective ingredient.** Career tools used entirely alone have a thin record; the presence of a counsellor is what reliably moves outcomes, and help works best when it builds both skill and motivation.
- **Confidence is causal, not soft.** The JOBS II programme improved both re-employment and mental health by building a sense of mastery and inoculating people against setbacks, with the largest gains for those most at risk.
- **For barrier groups, place then train.** Supported employment, which places people quickly and supports them afterwards, roughly doubles competitive employment for people with significant barriers.
- **Speed matters.** The longer someone is unemployed, the harder re-entry becomes, so anything that restores momentum early works against that scarring.
- **Re-training pays off slowly, and should say so.** Study reduces job search while it lasts, and the returns arrive over years, not months. Shorter training aimed at real demand performs better than long routes chosen from a catalogue.
- **Support reaches the confident first.** Left alone, re-training help and funding are taken up most by those who need them least.
- **Digital-only help widens the gap.** People with low digital confidence benefit least from self-service tools; blended human and digital help is needed to reach them.
- **Different situations, different paths.** There is no single funnel that fits everyone, and the evidence itself says so.

The design follows from that. Instead of analysing a jobseeker and pushing them into a predetermined path, the system lets the person's situation find the right help, across every route, with a human beside them. The help comes to the person, not the person to a funnel.

## Appendix B: Three Weeks in the Life

**Sara is 52 and has been out of work for fourteen months. Her confidence is low and she has started to avoid the search altogether.**

**Monday and Tuesday.** She opens the hub. One warm greeting and one next step: build her profile. She uploads an old CV and two letters, then Lilly asks about her last role, one question at a time. Where her old documents already hold the answer, she confirms it; where they do not, she answers out loud. No blank page. Over two short sessions her profile and CV are done.

**Wednesday.** Her start page shows six new jobs found overnight, each with the reason. She picks two and cuts one, "too far to travel", and agrees the system should skip jobs that far away from now on.

**Thursday.** For each job an employer brief, a tailored CV and a letter are waiting. One advert asks for experience training new staff, so she gets one question, and answers that she onboarded the summer temps for six years. It will be there for every application after this one. The reviewers flag one missing keyword, she adds it, and she presses send.

**Friday.** Her coach opens Sara's case and the week is already there, so the meeting is about how Sara is feeling. A request has quietly gone to another coach whose network reaches the company, and that coach offers to put in a word.

**The week after.** An invitation arrives. She practises out loud with the interview trainer, three times, alone in her kitchen. By the third try she does not freeze.

**Three weeks later Sara has an offer.** The system did not replace her coach. It made sure everything between the meetings happened, and that every word she sent was hers.

---

**Johan is 47 and was made redundant after twenty-two years in a printing plant that closed. He does not need a better CV. He needs a direction.**

**Monday.** The Transition Compass asks him, in plain spoken questions, why he is here and what he can carry financially. It notes that this change was not his choice, and his coach sees that too.

**Tuesday.** The Direction Explorer shows three realistic moves built on what he knows: machine operation, quality control and maintenance. The Prior Learning Check shows that part of his maintenance experience can be formally recognised. Beside every direction: apply now anyway.

**Wednesday.** He picks maintenance to test. The Education Matcher shows a short tuition-free course nearby that includes a placement. The system drafts a message to a maintenance technician his coach knows, and finds a taster day.

**Thursday.** His study funding pack is assembled: the checklist, the details, and the motivation his coach has reviewed. When the window opens, he files in minutes.

**Johan's week did not end in a job.** It ended in a direction he had tested, a course that costs him nothing, a placement that could become a job, and a coach who signed off on it.

---

**Amina is 38. She came to Sweden four years ago, speaks three languages, and worked as a pharmacy assistant before she moved. She does not own a computer. Everything she does after Monday is on her phone, in plain Swedish, a few minutes at a time.**

**Monday.** She meets her coach, who opens HelloLilly on the office screen and starts the career intake with her, together, one spoken question at a time. For the first time her experience is being written down properly, by her, in her words, and her CV begins to take shape from the answers.

**Tuesday.** She carries on with the intake on her phone, a few questions at a time. She talks through the gaps the adverts point to, four years away from pharmacy work and Swedish she is still building, and her answers become honest CV lines and a sentence for her letters. On the bus home she watches a four-minute clip on how a Swedish CV is read.

**Wednesday.** By day three her CV is done and her plan is agreed with her coach: pharmacy as the goal, and jobs she can apply for now, in warehouses and elderly care. Her first applications go out, and from now on a few go out every day. One form asks why she wants the job; she says it out loud, and the answer comes back clear and ready to paste. In the evening she learns how Swedish pharmacies are organised: the big chains, the roles from pharmacy technician to pharmacist, and what each one asks for.

**Thursday.** A few more applications. Plan Analysis shows that her foreign training can be assessed, and that a short tuition-free course with a placement is the realistic route back. She applies for the course and keeps the job applications running beside it. Her LinkedIn profile is drafted from her CV.

**Friday.** A few more applications. She posts for the first time: why she wants back into pharmacy, talked through and then written. The Contact Planner suggests five pharmacy managers in her region who post about their work. She leaves a short, thoughtful comment on each and sends five short hellos, each drafted for her, edited by her and sent by her. Her coach finds, through Network Match, that a colleague in another office knows the manager of a pharmacy chain that takes trainees, and the colleague makes the introduction. Through the Expertise Exchange, a fellow jobseeker who was a photographer takes her profile photo; in return, Amina answers a question in the community about working in a pharmacy.

**Amina's week was not about a CV.** It was a plan by day three, applications every day after it, three routes at once (work, education and a placement), and help from people she had never met. That is the infrastructure working.

## Appendix C: What Already Runs, in Detail

Three parts of the system already run in the author's own tooling. They are described here in full so the main paper can stay at the level of the whole system.

### 1. The application route


- **The start page.** Job search begins on a morning page with tabs for *New today*, *Questions*, *Send*, *Cuts* and **Ansökningskoll**, where every sent application shows its status. One clear next action at a time.
- **Job Search (Jobbsök).** The person keeps their own list of job titles, plus a broad catch-all search and an optional direction track that only adds options. Many sources are combined so each job appears once. Only new jobs are fetched each day. If a site blocks access, the search stops and tries again tomorrow; it never works around a block.
- **Filtering that learns.** Every cut job carries a named reason and can be overruled. When the person cuts a job by hand, their reason becomes a draft rule; once they accept it, it joins their preferences.
- **Match Analysis (Matchanalys).** Each job gets two scores, always with their explanation: how likely the person is to **like** the job, and how well they **fit** what the employer wants. Every requirement in the advert is checked against the person's evidence, with the source named. The scores sort a long list; they are never a verdict on the person. When a requirement cannot honestly be met, the thread is handed to the Learn and re-skill route instead of ending in a dead end.
- **Go or Cut.** The person decides. Undecided jobs wait until tomorrow.
- **Employer brief.** For each chosen job, a brief with a fixed core (culture, way of working, stability, career path, hiring risk) plus topics chosen for the profession: shift patterns and staffing levels for a nurse, safety record and permanent versus agency work for a factory worker. Open sources only, and it says what it could not read.
- **Tailored CV and Cover Letter (Anpassat CV, Personligt brev).** Starts from the closest CV version and uses confirmed lines only. Sharper wording is proposed, never imposed. The letter names one concrete link to the company, its industry, a competitor or the phase the organisation is in, and can help address what the person worries about, such as a gap, age, a career change or limited Swedish, honestly and in their favour.
- **Gap interview.** When the advert asks for something the bank does not cover, the system asks one question at a time. Accepted answers go into the person's bank, so every application makes the next one stronger.
- **Application Check (Ansökningsgranskning).** Three reviewers read the application before it is sent: one as an applicant tracking system, one as a recruiter, one as an HR consultant. They point out problems and never rewrite. It is a simulation, not access to the employer's own system.
- **Final check.** A separate checker compares every line and figure with the person's confirmed bank. A line that is not in the bank blocks the application until it is fixed or confirmed. Everything else is advice.
- **Application Questions (Ansökningsfrågor).** When the form asks questions of its own, the person pastes each one into a chat beside the application. The answer comes from the person's own material, at the length the form allows, ready to paste. When the material holds no answer, the tool asks rather than invents. Every kept answer is saved to an answer bank, so the next form is quicker.
- **Send.** The person sends. The application appears in Ansökningskoll, and a job already applied for never comes back.

### 2. The interview preparation dashboard

Built and used for real interviews. For each interview the tool produces a two-part dashboard:

- **The answer dashboard.** Everything the person has prepared, searchable and filterable: the opening pitch, the company research, the analysis of the job, the analysis of the recruiter or hiring manager, the gaps to fill and how to bridge them, the experience and stories to highlight, the anticipated questions with strong answers, and the questions to ask.
- **The interactive advert.** The job advert itself, where the key phrases are clickable and each one opens the person's prepared answer or angle for it.

The research behind it covers the company, the market, the role, the real employer behind a recruiter, and the people the person will meet, with the weighting adjusted for the stage: warmth and motivation for a recruiter screen, strategy and sharp questions for a hiring manager. In an online interview, the dashboard sits open beside the call as live support. For an interview in person, it is the preparation tool and a printout to bring. Every bold claim in the CV becomes something prepared for.

### 3. LinkedIn search and the contact vault

Built and running on another platform the author operates:

- **People search.** Finding people on LinkedIn by company, role and industry from public profile information, which is what the Contact Planner needs to build its target lists by role.
- **Post search.** Finding what those people have recently posted, which gives the gentlest approach, a thoughtful comment on something they wrote, something real to respond to, and gives the LinkedIn Plan people and posts worth engaging with.
- **The contact vault.** A private, encrypted store of a person's own downloaded LinkedIn connections and content, kept on their own computer, which can answer whether a contact reaches a given company with a yes or a no and nothing more. This is the basis for Coach Contacts and Network Match, and the same machinery serves a jobseeker's own Network Map.

All three use public information or the owner's own exported data only. Nothing behind a login is scraped, and nothing is ever posted or sent automatically.
