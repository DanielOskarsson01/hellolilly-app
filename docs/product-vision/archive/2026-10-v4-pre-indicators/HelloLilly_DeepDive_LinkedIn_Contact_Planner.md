# HelloLilly - The Personal Brand Studio: LinkedIn and the Contact Planner

**Deep-dive companion to the Jobseeker Product Vision, Version 4 · October 2026 · Confidential draft**

The tool that helps a jobseeker who has never thought of themselves as a "brand" to build one: a LinkedIn profile created from their CV, a simple brand plan with daily and weekly tasks, a way to find and add the right new contacts, and a queue of their posts worth commenting on. A CRM and a media plan in one, built for beginners and for people who find all of this hard.

---

## Opening

For most long-term jobseekers, LinkedIn is not an opportunity. It is a source of anxiety. The profile is empty or ten years old. Everyone else seems to be celebrating promotions. Posting feels like showing off, and writing to a stranger feels like begging. So the person does nothing, and the one channel where many jobs are really filled, through people who know people, stays closed.

The advice they get makes it worse: "build your personal brand", "network more", "be visible". It sounds like something for marketers and extroverts. For an introvert, for someone whose confidence has been worn down by rejection, for someone writing in their second language, it is a wall.

HelloLilly answers with one tool, the **Personal Brand Studio (Varumärkesstudion)**. It treats a personal brand as three modest things: being **findable** by the right people, being **remembered** for one clear thing, and being **easy to help**. And it turns all three into a plan of small, concrete tasks, each prepared so that the person only has to read, adjust, copy, paste and press send.

The approach borrows from what has been measured to work on LinkedIn for a professional platform the author runs: people's own original posts beat shared content, naming and tagging real people widens reach, comments cost nothing and are where most relationships start, and depth beats volume. Here it is translated from a company growth strategy into a beginner's path for one person looking for work.

The division of labour is the same as everywhere in HelloLilly. **The system carries the load**: it builds the profile from the CV, proposes the plan, finds the people, surfaces their posts, drafts the comments and messages, and keeps track. **The person makes the choices**: what to say, whom to contact, when, and whether to send at all. **The coach does the human work**: encouragement, a second look at the scary message, and an introduction where they can make one.

---

## 1. The Problem

- **How it is:** Many jobs are filled through a recommendation, an introduction or a conversation that started on LinkedIn. Long-term jobseekers often have the thinnest networks, an outdated profile, and the greatest fear of reaching out. Introverts, people with social anxiety, people out of work a long time and people writing in a second language are hit hardest.
- **What is missing:** One tool that builds a credible profile, plans a small and steady presence, finds the right people, and prepares every comment and message so it feels safe to send.
- **The effect:** The person competes only through open adverts, the most crowded channel, where a gap in the CV counts most and who they are counts least.
- **Scenario:** A team leader at the company you want to work for posted last week about a problem you solved for years in your old job. You could have commented. You did not, because you did not know what to write, and you were afraid it would look strange. Three weeks later the company hires someone the team leader "got talking to on LinkedIn".

## 2. The Studio at a Glance

The Personal Brand Studio has four modules that work as one. It opens only after the CV Builder, because every word in the profile comes from the person's confirmed facts.

| Module | What it does | The person's job |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Profile Builder** | Creates the LinkedIn profile from the CV, field by field | Copy each part in, approve the result |
| **2. Brand Plan** | A personal media plan: daily, every-other-day and weekly tasks, with ready drafts | Do the tasks that feel possible today |
| **3. Contact Finder and CRM** | Finds relevant new people, keeps track of every contact and every step | Choose whom to add, send the prepared notes |
| **4. Engagement Queue** | Shows recent posts from the person's contacts and targets that are worth a comment, with a draft | Pick one or two, adjust, comment |

The person sees one screen each day: **today's tasks**, with everything prepared.

| Today | Example |
|---|---|
| Comment on 2 posts | Maria (team leader, Nordic Cold) posted about shift handovers; a draft comment is ready |

| This week's post (optional) | An idea from your own experience, and whom to mention |

## 3. Module 1: Profile Builder

**Why:** a complete profile is the foundation for everything else, and it is the step most people can finish in one sitting with help.

The Profile Builder creates the LinkedIn profile **from the CV**. The same confirmed facts are rewritten the way LinkedIn is read, never with anything that is not in the person's fact bank. The person copies each part into LinkedIn, guided field by field, with a plain-language explanation of where each part goes.

| Part of the profile | What the tool prepares | The rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| **Photo** | A clear photo from Image Studio, "same face, better photograph" | Face visible, plain background, looks like you on a good day |
| **Background image** | A simple, calm image related to the person's field | Better plain than busy |
| **Headline** | What you do or want to do, and for whom, with the words recruiters search for | "Warehouse team leader · cold chain and logistics · looking for my next role in the Jönköping area" beats "Unemployed" |
| **About** | Four or five short sentences in the first person: what you do, what you are good at, one proof, what you are looking for | Your voice, plain Swedish or English, no buzzwords |
| **Experience** | Each role with two or three results-focused lines from the CV | The same facts as the CV, never more |
| **Skills** | Skills from confirmed tasks, matched to the words in adverts for the target role | Ten good skills beat fifty random ones |
| **Education and certificates** | Including courses completed through the Learn and Re-skill route | Recent learning signals momentum |
| **Open to work** | Advice on the choice: visible only to recruiters, or the public banner | The person decides; both are fine |
| **Featured** | Optional: a post, a certificate, a project | Only if there is something worth showing |
| **Recommendations** | A prepared request to two or three people who have seen the person work | One real recommendation beats many skills |
| **Language** | Swedish, English or both, depending on the target market | Both from the same facts |

The result is checked by the LinkedIn Helper before the person copies it in: *ready*, *ready with one change*, or *not yet*. When the CV changes, for example after a new course, the Profile Builder suggests the matching profile update.

## 4. Module 2: Brand Plan

**Why:** a small, steady presence makes the person remembered, and the people they later contact will recognise their name.

The Brand Plan is a media plan for one person. It turns "build your brand" into a calendar of small tasks, sized to the person's energy.

### The task rhythm

| Cadence | Tasks | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily** (weekdays) | Comment on one or two posts from the Engagement Queue | 10 minutes |
| **Every other day** | Add three to five new relevant contacts, each with a short note | 10 minutes |
| **Weekly** | Follow up contacts who accepted; reconnect with one former colleague; review replies | 15 minutes |
| **Every one or two weeks** | One original post, if and when it feels right | 20 minutes |
| **Monthly** | Profile check-up; ask for one recommendation; look at what worked | 20 minutes |

About an hour a week in total. The plan adapts: the person chooses an intensity (**gentle**, **steady** or **active**), and on a hard day the plan offers one small task, or none, and that counts. Every task appears in Progress Support as a small step, never as a streak to keep.

### What works, in plain words

- **Your own words beat shared content.** A short original post beats resharing an article or a company post.
- **Comments cost nothing and build the most.** A thoughtful comment is seen by the other person's network, and it is where most new relationships begin. For a beginner, comments are the main activity.
- **Naming real people widens reach.** A post that mentions and tags a real person is shown to their network too. Measured on a professional page the author runs, posts tagging a named person reached noticeably more people.
- **One a day, at most.** A second post on the same day takes reach from the first. For a jobseeker, one post every week or two is plenty.
- **Depth beats volume.** One honest post with something only you know is worth more than five generic ones.
- **Stay for the first hour.** Post when you can answer the first comments.

### The two-part test for every post

> **1. Does it contain something only you could know?** A lesson from your trade, something you noticed on a course, a real situation you handled.
> **2. Does it end with a real question?** Something specific people in your field could answer.

A post that passes both is an invitation. A post that only tells is a broadcast.

### Post ideas the plan suggests

The plan proposes ideas from the person's own fact bank and recent activity, never invented experiences:

| Type | Example idea | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **The lesson from experience** | "Twenty years in cold-chain logistics taught me one thing about temperature logs that no system catches..." | Shows expertise without bragging |
| **The observation** | "Three care homes I visited this month all had the same problem at shift change..." | Shows you think about the work |
| **The learning note** | "Week three of my electrician course. The thing that surprised me most..." | Momentum and direction |
| **The thank-you** | "I want to thank @Anna, who explained load planning better in ten minutes than any manual..." | Generous, tags a real person, travels |
| **The building-on post** | "As @Johan put so well in his post about warehouse safety, ... I would add one thing from my years on the floor..." | Joins a conversation that already has an audience |
| **The real question** | "For those in health tech: what do you wish people coming from nursing understood on day one?" | Invites help without asking for a job |
| **The clear ask (rarely)** | "I am looking for my next role as a team leader in logistics around Jönköping. If you know someone I should talk to, I would be grateful." | Makes it easy to help |

### House rules

- No "Agree?", no "Thoughts?", no bait for comments.
- Hashtags sparingly, or not at all.
- One idea per post; no two posts on the same day.
- Nothing untrue, nothing negative about a former employer.
- Your words. The system suggests ideas, shapes, shortens and checks; it never posts and never invents an experience.

## 5. Module 3: Contact Finder and CRM

**Why:** the people who matter most in a job search are often strangers, and growing a relevant network steadily is what makes every later post, search and request reach further.

### Choose the targets

The person chooses what they are aiming at, in any combination:

- **Companies** they would like to work at (from the Company List, Companies by Area or Job Radar);
- **Industries** they want to work in;
- **Roles** they want to have;
- **A region**, if location matters.

### Find relevant new people

For each target, the Contact Finder searches public profile and post information and suggests people, grouped by role, with a short reason why each one is relevant:

| Who | Why they matter | What they usually respond to |
|---|---|---|
| **Senior leaders** | They set direction and can open any door | Brief, relevant, respectful; best reached through a comment first |
| **Hiring managers** | They decide | Evidence you understand their team's problem |
| **HR and talent** | They run the process | Clarity on role, location and availability |
| **Recruiters** | They fill roles every week | A clear profile and a clear ask |
| **Future team members** | They know the real job, and they refer | Genuine curiosity about their work |
| **Industry people** | They know who is hiring before the advert | A real question about the field |
| **People like you** | Others who made the same move or work in the same trade | Shared experience |

It also suggests **warm contacts**: former colleagues, people from a course or placement, members of the HelloLilly community in the same field, and people from the person's own Network Map.

### Add them, gently

Each suggestion comes with a short, personal connection note, drafted from the person's facts and, where possible, from something the other person recently posted:

> "Hi Maria, I enjoyed your post about shift handovers. I have led night shifts in cold-chain logistics for many years and would be glad to follow what you share."

The person reads it, adjusts it, copies it into LinkedIn and sends.

### The CRM: every contact, every step

Every contact is kept in one simple list, with its stage and its next step:

| Stage | Meaning | Next step the CRM prepares |
|---|---|---|
| **Suggested** | Found as relevant | Follow, or comment first |
| **Warmed** | The person has commented on their post | A connection request with a note |
| **Connected** | They accepted | A thank-you, or "one simple question" |
| **In conversation** | They replied | Ten minutes of advice, or a follow-up |
| **Helping** | They offered advice, an introduction or a referral | A thank-you, and an update later |
| **Resting** | No reply after one follow-up | Nothing; respect the silence |

Contacts can be linked to companies, jobs and applications at any time, so the person sees who they know at the company where they just applied.

### The approach menu

From the gentlest to the boldest. The person picks what feels possible today.

| Approach | What it is | Courage needed |
|---|---|---|
| **Comment on their post** | A thoughtful comment, no ask at all | Lowest |
| **Connect with a short note** | A personal two-line note | Low |
| **One simple question** | "I would love to work in your industry. Would you mind if I asked you one simple question?" | Medium |
| **Ask for ten minutes of advice** | A short conversation about their field, not about a job | Medium to high |
| **Ask for an introduction or a recommendation** | Only once there is a relationship | Highest |

The order matters. A recommendation request to a stranger rarely works; the same request after a comment, a connection and a short conversation often does.

## 6. Module 4: Engagement Queue

**Why:** commenting is the single most valuable and least risky thing a beginner can do on LinkedIn, but finding the right posts and knowing what to write is exactly where people stop.

Each day the Engagement Queue shows a few recent posts worth answering, from:

- new contacts the person has just added or wants to warm up;
- people at target companies;
- people in the target field;
- existing connections who posted something relevant.

For each post it shows why it is worth a comment, and a draft in one of five shapes, written from the person's own experience:

1. **Add from experience:** "In my years running night shifts, we saw the same thing. What worked for us was..."
2. **A precise question:** one specific, answerable question about the post.
3. **A respectful other view:** "I read this a little differently, for one reason..."
4. **An extension:** "This also holds for small warehouses, with one twist..."
5. **A thank-you with substance:** what in the post was useful, and why.

The person picks one or two, adjusts, copies, comments. When a comment leads to a reply, the CRM moves that contact to the next stage and prepares the next step.

## 7. Designed for Introverts and Beginners

- **No calls required.** Everything can happen in writing, at the person's own pace.
- **Scripts, not improvisation.** Every comment and message is prepared; the person only has to approve it.
- **Small numbers.** Five new contacts is a good day; one comment is a good day too.
- **"Not today" is valid.** The plan offers something smaller, or nothing.
- **Kind framing of silence.** Most messages get no answer for reasons that have nothing to do with the person. The Studio says so, and shows the replies that did come.
- **A coach on hand.** The scary message can go to a coach for a second look first.
- **The LinkedIn Helper on everything.** Every text can be checked before sending: *send*, *send with one change*, or *hold*. After *send* it offers nothing more.

---

## 8. Safety, Honesty and Boundaries

- **The person always sends.** Nothing is ever posted, sent, liked or connected automatically. The Studio stops at the send button.
- **Public information only.** People and posts are found from public information. Nothing behind a login is ever scraped, and the person's account is never automated.
- **No mass messaging.** Small daily numbers; generic outreach harms the person's reputation.
- **True claims only.** The profile, posts and messages use the person's confirmed facts and own words.
- **Respect for the other person.** One follow-up at most; silence and "no" are respected.
- **No vanity scores.** No follower targets, no "your messages landed well" score.

The people search and post search behind the Contact Finder and the Engagement Queue already run on another platform the author operates, reused here under these rules.

## 11. A Thirty-Day Starter Plan

| Week | Focus | The Studio prepares |
|---|---|---|
| **Week 1** | Get findable | The full profile from the CV; the photo; two recommendation requests |
| **Week 2** | Listen and comment | Twenty people and ten companies to follow; one or two comments a day from the queue |
| **Week 3** | Connect | The first targets; five new contacts every other day with notes; five former colleagues to reconnect with |
| **Week 4** | First post and first ask | One post idea that passes the two-part test, tagging one real person; one "one simple question" message |

After thirty days, most people have a complete profile, a growing network in the right field, a handful of real conversations, and the beginnings of a name people recognise. That is a personal brand, at the size a jobseeker needs.

## Appendix: Example Messages

Shown in English here; in the product they are prepared in Swedish or English, from the person's own facts and the other person's real post.

**A comment that adds from experience**
"Thank you for this, Maria. In my eight years leading night shifts at a cold store, the handover was always where things slipped. What helped us was a two-minute standing check at the temperature log. Have you seen anything similar work in larger sites?"

**A connection request after a comment**
"Hi Maria, I commented on your post about shift handovers. I have worked in cold-chain logistics for many years and would be glad to follow what you share."

**One simple question**
"Hi Erik, thank you for connecting. I would love to work in health tech after twelve years as a nurse. Would you mind if I asked you one simple question about how you made the move?"

**Ten minutes of advice**
"Hi Sara, I have really appreciated your posts about warehouse automation. I am exploring roles in the field and would value ten minutes of your perspective on what employers look for right now. If it suits you, any time next week would work for me. If not, no problem at all."

**A recommendation request, to someone who knows your work**
"Hi Johan, I hope you are well. I am looking for my next role as a team leader, and a short recommendation from someone who saw my work would mean a lot. If you are willing, I would be happy to suggest a few points you could mention. Thank you either way."

**A thank-you after a conversation**
"Thank you for your time today, Anna. Your point about certificates was exactly what I needed to hear. I will look into the course you mentioned and let you know how it goes."
