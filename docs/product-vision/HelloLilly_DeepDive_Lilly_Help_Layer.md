# HelloLilly - Lilly and Help Right Now

**Deep-dive companion to the Jobseeker Product Vision, Version 4 · October 2026 · Confidential draft**

The help layer that sits beside every task in HelloLilly: a showroom for more help, a column of widgets that pushes forward the help that actually exists for whatever is happening on the page, and an assistant, Lilly, who explains, finds the way and hands over, without ever acting on her own.

---

## Opening

The Version 4 vision makes a strong claim: the most important part of HelloLilly is not any single tool. It is the way every tool, person, resource, course, coach and piece of knowledge is crosslinked to whatever the jobseeker is doing at that moment. **The tools are the visible layer. The crosslinking is the system.**

The help layer is where that claim becomes something a person can see and touch. It is the right-hand column on every work screen, and it has two parts:

- **Help right now (Hjälp just nu)**, the floor: a column of widgets that follow the page. On a job, they show the employer research, who has contacts there, the people at the company and what they have posted lately, the coach who knows this job or employer best, and the course that closes the gap; on a CV step, the template, the clip and the right coach. Only real items, never filler.
- **Lilly**, the voice above the floor: the assistant the person says hello to. The product is HelloLilly; Lilly is the one you say hello to. She explains any tool, finds the way, answers questions about the person's own situation by pointing to what is actually in it, and hands over to a coach when judgement is needed.

Most systems organise information by category and leave the person to search. The help layer organises help by situation and brings it to the person. For someone without digital confidence, that is the difference between help that exists and help that is used.

---

## 1. The Problem

- **How it is:** Help is spread across menus, libraries, forums, websites and people. The person has to know that it exists, where it is, and what it is called, and has to leave the task to find it.
- **What is missing:** Help that appears beside the work, at the moment it is needed, and a guide who can explain anything in plain Swedish without the person having to know the right question.
- **The effect:** The people who most need help are the least likely to find it. Good resources sit unused, and people abandon tasks at the first point of confusion.
- **Scenario:** You are writing the "About" section of your CV and have no idea how to start. Somewhere in the system there is a great template, a two-minute video and a coach who is brilliant at exactly this. You do not know any of them exist, so you close the laptop.

---

## 2. Help Right Now: Widgets That Follow the Page

### The idea

Help right now is a **showroom for more help**: guidance and help that would otherwise be lost and never seen. The help the person most probably needs at that moment is pushed forward. It is not a list of links. It is a column of **widgets**, each holding a few real items, chosen for exactly what is happening on the page the person is looking at. The idea is taken from the cross-section discovery plan built for another platform the author runs, where every page surfaces related news, jobs, companies, events and discussions from across the whole site. Here the same engine connects the person's task to everything in HelloLilly that can help with it.

Most systems organise help by category: a library here, a forum there, courses somewhere else. Each section is optimised for itself, and none of them talk to each other. A job with a certificate gap and a three-week course that closes it are clearly related, but nothing connects them. Help right now does.

### The widgets

Each widget has a type, a small maximum number of items, and only ever shows things that exist.

| Widget | What each item shows | Maximum items |
|---|---|---|
| **Next step** | The single smallest useful action, from Progress Support | 1 |
| **Jobs** | Title, employer, place, why it fits, how fresh | 3 |
| **Courses and routes** | Course, length, tuition-free or not, start date, place, the gap it closes | 3 |
| **Placements** | Employer, type (placement, work trial, supported job), place | 2 |
| **Templates and examples** | A coach-approved template or an anonymised example for this exact step | 2 |
| **Clips and guides** | A short video or guide from the Tutorial Library, with its length | 2 |
| **Discussions** | A live community thread, shown as people, not as a link (see below) | 2 |
| **Ask the coach who knows** | The coach the system judges to know most about this job, field or employer, with the reason, and a way to ask them | 1 |
| **Who has contacts there** | People in the person's own contacts at the company, and a quiet request to check whether a coach's network reaches it; only a yes or no ever leaves a coach's vault | 3 |
| **People at the company** | People by role at the employer, found by the Contact Planner from public profiles | 3 |
| **Their recent posts** | What those people have posted lately, as a natural first thing to comment on | 2 |
| **Company research** | The employer brief: culture, way of working, stability, hiring risk, and what it could not read | 1 |
| **Companies** | Companies related to the job, the direction or the region | 3 |
| **Funding and rules** | The funding or rule that applies here, as a dated fact | 2 |

### What the person sees, page by page

The mix of widgets follows the kind of page:

| Kind of page | What Help right now shows |
|---|---|
| **The hub and overview pages** | The next step, and what is new since last time: new jobs, a reply in a thread, a course with an approaching deadline |
| **A list page** (jobs, courses, contacts) | Help for the list itself: how to sort it, what the filters mean, one related clip |
| **A detail page** (one job, one course, one company, one interview) | The full cross-route discovery: everything in the system related to this exact item and this person |
| **A working page** (writing a CV line, a letter, a post) | Help for the step in hand: a template, an example, a clip, the coach for this |

### How items are chosen

**Meaning, not tags.** Every item in HelloLilly, a job, a course, a clip, a thread, a company, a coach's strengths, is read and understood by AI when it is added, and compared with everything else. The best matches are stored in advance, so a page loads instantly with its help already chosen. A course tagged "vocational, care" and a job tagged "elderly care, nights" are matched because they mean related things, not because their tags happen to agree.

**The person's situation counts too.** The match is made against both the page and the person's case: their target roles, their gaps, their region, their plan, what they have already done. The same job page shows different help to a person with the forklift licence and to one without.

**Which coach to ask.** For Ask the coach who knows, the system reads every coach's profile: their fields, the kinds of people they have placed, the employers and industries they know. It picks the coach who knows most about this exact job or employer and shows why. The coach can always say no, and the person never sees anyone's private contacts.

**Freshness by type.** Not everything ages the same way, so each type decays at its own pace:

| Item type | Freshness rule |
|---|---|
| **Jobs** | Fade within weeks; removed entirely when closed or past the deadline |
| **Courses** | Rank by how soon they start and when applications close; removed when the window has passed |
| **Placements** | Removed when filled |
| **Funding and rules** | Shown only for the current year; refused if the source has retired the figure |
| **Discussions** | Freshness comes from the **last reply**, not the date the thread started |
| **Templates, clips and guides** | Evergreen, until replaced |
| **Companies and coaches** | Rarely go stale |

**Things that never appear.** A closed job. A course whose application window has passed. Something the person has already done or dismissed. An item related to itself. Duplicates. Anything a coach has flagged. And never a slot filled just to look busy: if a widget has nothing real, it is not shown.

**When there is not enough.** If meaning-based matching finds too little, the widget falls back to simpler matching on categories, then to the newest relevant items, and if there is still nothing, it is hidden. For discussions there is a better fourth option, below.

### Discussions: people, not links

A discussion is not content to read. It is people to join. So the Discussions widget follows its own rules, taken from the same cross-section plan:

- **Alive beats similar.** A thread that is fairly relevant but has eight people talking beats a perfectly relevant thread with one post.
- **Freshness from activity.** A thread started months ago with a reply yesterday is fresh; a thread started yesterday with no replies is not.
- **No empty rooms.** A thread with no replies is never shown.
- **Show the room.** "Three people moving from nursing into health tech are talking about first-day surprises, last reply two hours ago" is an invitation. "Related discussion: health tech" is a link. The widget shows how many people, how recently, and a one-line taste, with names or masked names as each member chose.
- **The empty state is an invitation.** When nobody has discussed this yet, the widget says so, and offers to ask the first question, with the page's context already attached and the option of a masked name.
- **The return path.** When someone joins a thread from the widget, the earlier participants are told someone replied. They come back, the thread grows, it surfaces on more pages, and more people find each other.

### No paid placement, ever

On the platform this engine was first designed for, partners may receive a small, capped visibility boost on objects such as company profiles, but never on conversations. HelloLilly goes further. **Nothing in Help right now can be paid for.** No course provider, staffing agency or employer can buy a place in a widget. Editorial may highlight something for merit; nobody may pay to place it. The help a person sees is chosen only for them.

### Why this does not compromise honesty

The widgets never write anything. They **select** existing items and show them as they are. Which widgets appear on which kind of page is decided by simple, predictable rules; the ranking of real items inside them uses meaning-based matching computed in advance. Nothing is generated, nothing is promised that does not exist, and the column grows richer by itself as HelloLilly fills with jobs, courses, clips, threads and coaches.

## 3. Lilly: The Assistant

### What Lilly does

- **Explains any tool.** What it does, what it needs, and what it will not do, in plain Swedish.
- **Finds the way.** "Open the cover letter with this job selected", as a link the person confirms, so nobody has to learn the menu.
- **Says what is next.** She asks Progress Support for the next step and passes it on. There is one brain for next steps, not two.
- **Explains a resource.** Any item in the Knowledge Hub or the Tutorial Library can be summarised or explained, so nobody has to read or watch everything.
- **Answers questions about the person's own situation, by citing it.** "You have three applications waiting" is said only because the case record says so, and she shows where. If it is not there, she does not say it.
- **Hands over.** When something needs judgement, she points to the right coach and says so.

### How Lilly behaves

Her posture is what makes her HelloLilly and not a chatbot:

1. **Quiet by default.** Present, collapsed, silent. She speaks when spoken to. The widgets are the only thing that offers help unprompted.
2. **One suggestion at a time.** Brief and clear. Never a menu where one answer will do.
3. **Honest about the person.** Any claim about the person cites their own facts or case, or she does not make it.
4. **Never blames.** She describes mechanisms, never traits: "the letter is missing two keywords", never "you tend to undersell yourself".
5. **Suggests, never acts.** She navigates, prefills and drafts hand-offs. The person confirms every change and every send. She never acts on her own.
6. **Upholds the other tools.** When the LinkedIn Helper has said *send*, and the person asks Lilly "but is it really okay?", she does not offer a fresh review. When a tool has refused something on grounds of truth, she does not argue around it. Permission to stop holds everywhere, or it holds nowhere.

### Spoken Lilly

Lilly is the natural home of the spoken mode. A person who does not want to type can talk to her, one question at a time, and she guides the next step. For many people, she is the way into the whole system.

---

## 4. What Lilly Will Not Do

- No constant chatter, no interruptions, no "just checking in", no streaks or engagement tricks.
- No actions on her own: no writing, sending or changing anything without the person's confirmation.
- No reading of the person's emotions, and no guesses about them presented as fact.
- No second opinion against another tool's verdict or refusal.
- No widget or item that does not lead to something real, and no paid placement.
- No long-term memory of conversations at the start; durable learning belongs to the learning layer, with its own rules.
- No therapy. Where a person is struggling, she points to the coach and to the right human help.

---

## 5. Why Lilly Matters for the People Furthest From Work

- **For the person who fears technology,** Lilly is a voice, not a menu.
- **For the person writing in a second language,** she explains in plain Swedish, not official Swedish.
- **For the person who does not know what they need,** the widgets bring the help to the task without the person having to ask.
- **For the person with low energy,** there is always exactly one next step, and nothing more.
- **For the coach,** Lilly answers the simple questions, so coach time goes to the human ones.

---

## 6. How the Help Layer Connects to Everything

The help layer is not a tool. It is the surface through which every other tool becomes reachable.

| Part of the system | How the help layer uses it |
|---|---|
| **Every page** | The widgets sit beside the task; Lilly is one tap away |
| **Knowledge Hub and Tutorial Library** | The source of templates, guides and clips; Lilly explains any item |
| **Community and Shared Bank** | Discussions and examples from people who hit the same wall |
| **Coach Network** | The right coach for the situation |
| **Progress Support** | The single source of "what next" |
| **Case Record and fact bank** | What Lilly may say about the person, always cited |
| **Research and employer briefs** | Research already done, shown beside the task |
| **Learn and Re-skill, Placement Finder** | The route that fits a gap |
| **Situation Guide and Letter Helper** | Plain-Swedish explanations of the person's situation |
| **Interview Preparation and LinkedIn** | The right war-room, clip or check at the right moment |
| **Learning layer** | Which help is actually used and leads to progress, measured in the learning layer's own records, never by watching the person |

---

## 7. How It Is Built

| Part | What it is | How it is built |
|---|---|---|
| **The widgets** | Rules decide which widgets appear on which kind of page; meaning-based matching, computed in advance, ranks the real items inside them | Built with the first work screens; richer as content grows; nothing generated, nothing paid for |
| **Lilly, first version** | Explain tools, find the way, what next, explain a resource, answer from the case with citations | A language model through an API, grounded in the system's own description of its tools, the person's case and the library; no bespoke model |
| **Spoken Lilly** | The same assistant by voice | Browser speech first |
| **Later** | Memory across conversations, richer guidance | Only through the learning layer, with consent and its own review |

The model decision is deliberate: an established model, made better by HelloLilly's own connected knowledge, citing its sources and able to forget a person on request. Personal data is never used to train a model.
