# Perplexity Deep Research prompt — Job-seeker pain points + digital help + re-skilling

> Paste everything below the line into Perplexity Deep Research. It's written to produce a cited report that can be re-consolidated with a parallel pass.

---

**Role & context.** You are a labour-market research analyst. I'm designing an AI-supported job-seeker support system for HelloLilly, Sweden's largest provider of matching services (offices in 60+ municipalities, results-based public funding, goal of matching 10,000 people to work or education by 2030). The system sits on top of coaches and an existing case-management system. I need an evidence-based research brief, not opinion.

**Geography & weighting.** Draw on international peer-reviewed research and meta-analyses, but add a distinct **Swedish-system layer** (Arbetsförmedlingen, results-based matching providers, komvux, yrkeshögskola/YH, SFI, labour-market training/AUB). Cover job seekers broadly, but **weight toward the harder-to-place populations** HelloLilly serves: long-term unemployed, newcomers/migrants with language barriers, neurodivergent people (ADHD, autism), older workers (55+), people with low digital literacy, and youth/NEETs.

**Your task.** Produce a structured, citation-backed report answering the questions below. For every major claim, cite the source (author/institution, year, link) and note the **evidence quality** (meta-analysis / RCT / observational / survey / expert opinion) and, where available, **effect sizes or magnitudes**. Flag where evidence is thin, mixed, or contested. Distinguish *correlation* from *causal* findings.

**Questions to answer:**

1. **Biggest pain points.** What does the research identify as the largest, most common barriers and pain points job seekers face? Rank by strength of evidence. Cover both *practical/execution* barriers (CV/application quality, search strategy, time, information overload) and *psychological* ones (self-efficacy, motivation, rejection, depression, learned helplessness). Cite the job-search self-regulation and self-efficacy literature (e.g. Kanfer et al. meta-analysis; Wanberg) and unemployment-wellbeing work (e.g. Jahoda; Paul & Moser meta-analysis).

2. **What actually works — interventions.** Which interventions have the strongest causal evidence for improving employment outcomes? Specifically address: structured job-search assistance/ALMPs (cite Card, Kluve & Weber meta-analyses), **job clubs / group-based support** (cite Azrin job club studies and successors), CBT-style/confidence interventions (e.g. JOBS Program — Caplan, Vinokur, Price), coaching/case-management, and behavioural "next-step"/implementation-intention nudges. Note what *doesn't* work or backfires (e.g. high-volume untargeted applications, pure information provision).

3. **Digital tools — evidence.** What's known about the effectiveness of *digital* job-search tools and AI assistance: online CV/application tools, ATS-optimisation, algorithmic job matching/recommendation, digital coaching, and the **digital divide** (who is excluded and why). Where is digital help proven vs hype?

4. **Re-skilling & education (treat as a major section).** What does the evidence say about **retraining, upskilling, vocational courses, and credentials** as a route back to work? Cover: returns to adult retraining/ALMP training programs and their *time lag* (lock-in effects), which kinds of training pay off vs don't, skill-gap and skills-mismatch measurement, and how to **evaluate which course/credential actually fits a given person's situation and target role** (labour-demand signals, skills taxonomies like ESCO/O*NET, credential ROI). Add the **Swedish layer**: komvux, yrkeshögskola (YH) outcomes, Arbetsförmedlingen labour-market training, validering (prior-learning recognition), and any data on course-to-employment rates.

5. **Population-specific barriers.** For each harder-to-place group (long-term unemployed, migrants/language, neurodivergent, 55+, low digital literacy, youth/NEET): what are the distinctive barriers and what interventions are shown to help that specific group?

6. **What's measurable / the data layer.** What outcome and process metrics does the research use (time-to-placement, placement rate, job quality/retention, self-efficacy scales)? What would a system need to capture to learn what works for whom?

7. **Gaps & contested areas.** Where is the evidence weak, outdated, or contradictory? What would you caution a product team against over-claiming?

**Output format.**
- Executive summary (10–15 bullet findings, each with a citation).
- One section per question above, with sub-findings + citations + evidence-quality tags.
- A ranked **"Top pain points"** table: pain point | evidence strength | best-supported intervention | source.
- A **"Re-skilling / course-fit"** section with the Swedish specifics called out.
- A **references list** with links, grouped by theme.
- A short **"what surprised the evidence vs common assumptions"** note.

Prioritise primary research, meta-analyses, and reputable institutional sources (OECD, EU/Cedefop, ILO, Swedish IFAU, SCB, Arbetsförmedlingen, peer-reviewed journals) over blogs or vendor marketing.
